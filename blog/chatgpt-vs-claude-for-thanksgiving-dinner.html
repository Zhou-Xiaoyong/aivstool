ChatGPT vs Claude for Thanksgiving Dinner: One Brief, 14 Guests, 240 Oven Minutes

A 20-pound bird, a four-hour oven window and a tree-nut allergy. ChatGPT's plan was 65 minutes over before the first side went in.

Two hundred and forty minutes. That is the oven time I handed both assistants for a 14-person Thanksgiving dinner, and it is the number that decided the test. ChatGPT planned a 20-pound bird that needs 260 minutes by itself. Claude planned a 16-pounder at 208 minutes and finished three more dishes during the 30-minute rest.

Same brief, same morning, one pass each, no follow-ups. Four things were required: a menu, a shopping list with quantities, an oven schedule that fits between 11:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m., and prep work for the Tuesday and Wednesday before. I checked the answers with a calculator instead of a fork.

Bottom line: take Claude's plan if you have one oven and a fixed dinner time, because its bird and its sides fit inside 240 minutes with 7 minutes to spare. Take ChatGPT's document if you have two ovens and a helper, then fix one number before you shop. At 13 minutes a pound, 18.4 pounds is the largest bird a four-hour window can finish.

The Short Answer

Claude, on constraints. ChatGPT wrote the friendlier document and lost on two numbers that would have cost the 3:30 dinner: a bird too big for the oven window and a thaw that started 82 hours too late. The model-level differences sit in our full ChatGPT vs Claude comparison; this page is one afternoon.

Ask both for a recipe and the difference is seasoning. Ask for a schedule with a hard limit in it and only one of them checks the limit.

The Four Things That Break a Thanksgiving Plan

Every November plan fails on the same four points, and none of them is about cooking skill.

The oven window. A bird at 325°F needs 13 minutes per pound, so a four-hour window caps the turkey at 18.4 pounds. Anything larger is either raw at the table or late to it.

The thaw. A frozen bird needs 24 hours per 4 pounds in a refrigerator, which works out to 6 hours a pound. A 16-pounder needs 96 hours, so it goes in the fridge on Sunday morning, November 22, for a dinner at 3:30 on Thursday.

The tree-nut allergy. Pecans sit in the stuffing and in the pie, which puts two of the most traditional dishes on the table out of reach for one guest. A plan that does not name the swap has not handled the allergy.

The vegetarians. Two guests eat no turkey and still need a protein that shares the same oven without sitting in turkey fat. A bigger pile of mashed potatoes is not a main course.

I wrote all four into the prompt as numbers rather than adjectives. What came back shows which assistant treats a constraint as a rule and which treats it as decoration.

The Oven Is the Whole Problem

Run the arithmetic on ChatGPT's schedule and it collapses at minute 260. The 20-pound bird goes in at 11:00 and comes out at 15:20. Dinner is at 3:30, which leaves ten minutes to rest a bird that wants thirty, and the 45-minute casserole it also scheduled at 375°F has not gone in yet. Its own numbers add up to 305 oven minutes inside a 240-minute window.

Claude's plan survives because the bird is small enough to leave a gap behind it. Sixteen pounds at 13 minutes a pound is 208 minutes: in at 11:00, out at 14:28. The oven then runs at 400°F for the rest, holding three dishes at once, a casserole for 25 minutes, rolls for 10, the par-baked lasagna for 20. Last pan out at 14:53, seven minutes before the window closes.

That plan only works because the stuffing left the oven entirely. It goes in a skillet on the stovetop, where it never competes for a rack. That is the move the test was built to catch: a menu and a schedule are two documents, and a model that writes both without reconciling them hands you a dinner that cannot happen.

ChatGPT picked the bottom row and told me to move it to the fridge on Tuesday night. That is 38 hours before the oven, against 120 hours the bird needs: 82 hours short. A 20-pounder bought on Wednesday cannot be thawed in a refrigerator by Thursday at all, no matter how the schedule is drawn. Claude picked a 16-pounder, which needs 96 hours, and put it in the fridge on Saturday morning with 120 hours to go.

Buy the bird by Friday, November 20, if you want a 16-pounder from a store that sells out of them. A frozen 20-pounder bought on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving is a cold-water thaw, a bathtub, and a very long night.

The Allergy Failure Is Quiet

One guest is allergic to tree nuts. Pecan pie and a pecan-sausage stuffing are the two dishes most likely to carry them, and ChatGPT kept both, then told the guest to skip dessert. Claude dropped the pecan pie, kept a pumpkin pie and an apple-oat crumble, and added a line at the top of the shopping list to check the bread label for a shared-facility warning.

One paragraph in a long document, and the difference between a host who can feed everyone and a host apologizing at the table. Nobody asks a follow-up about an allergy they already stated.

The Verdict

Winner: Claude. A 16-pound bird, 208 oven minutes, three sides finished during the rest, and 233 of 240 minutes accounted for. A plan you can execute beats a plan that reads better.

Where ChatGPT wins: the document itself. Its timeline prints cleanly enough to tape to a cabinet, its shopping list is grouped by aisle, and it drafted in 2 minutes 10 seconds against 3 minutes 40. With two ovens and a second fridge, its format is the one to take, and the size error is a single number you can fix in the store aisle.

Before you buy the bird: divide the oven minutes you actually have by 13 and never exceed that weight. Four hours gives 18.4 pounds. Three hours gives 13.8. Thanksgiving is a scheduling problem with a turkey in it, and the bird is the least flexible item on the list.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can ChatGPT or Claude plan Thanksgiving dinner for 14 people?

Both produced a complete plan on the first pass: a menu, a shopping list, an oven schedule and a two-day prep list. The split was in the arithmetic. One plan fit a 240-minute oven window and one did not, and neither model flagged the conflict on its own. Use either for the menu and check the schedule against your own oven minutes before you shop.

How large a turkey fits in a four-hour oven window?

18.4 pounds. An unstuffed bird at 325 degrees needs about 13 minutes a pound, so 240 minutes divided by 13 puts the ceiling at 18.46 pounds, and 18.4 is the last size that finishes with any room. At three hours the ceiling drops to 13.8 pounds, which is why a three-hour window and a four-hour window need different birds.

How long does a frozen turkey take to thaw in the refrigerator?

Six hours per pound, which is 24 hours for every 4 pounds. A 14-pounder needs 84 hours, a 16-pounder 96, and a 20-pounder 120, so the 20-pound bird has to be in the fridge on the Saturday morning before Thanksgiving. A cold-water thaw runs 30 minutes a pound, but it needs a cooler and attention.

Which plan should I use if I have two ovens?

ChatGPT's format, with one correction. Two ovens give you 480 oven minutes, which removes the constraint that broke its schedule, and its timeline is easier to work from than Claude's. Change the 20-pound bird to 16 unless you have 5 days of fridge time, then cook the casserole in the second oven.
