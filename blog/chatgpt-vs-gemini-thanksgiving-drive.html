ChatGPT vs Gemini for Thanksgiving Travel: One 355-Mile Drive, Timed

Leave Chicago at 3:10 p.m. on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving and you reach Columbus at 10:23, not 8:30. One plan did the time-zone subtraction.

3:10 p.m. on Wednesday, November 25. School pickup done, dog in the back, 355 miles to Columbus. On paper that is a five-and-a-half-hour drive. On the dashboard clock it is seven hours and thirteen minutes, and the two assistants I asked disagreed about the arrival by 1 hour 55 minutes.

ChatGPT said 8:30 p.m. Gemini said 10:25 p.m. The stopwatch answer is 10:23 p.m. Eastern, and that number comes from the same three legs and two stops either model could have used.

Both got the same brief with the constraints spelled out: a 2019 CR-V, two adults, a four-year-old, a seven-year-old, a 35-pound dog, a 3:10 p.m. departure from Logan Square, dinner in Grandview Heights the next day. What I left out of the prompt was the time zone, because nobody writes “remember that most of Indiana is Eastern” when they ask for directions.

Bottom line: take Gemini's plan and put 10:23 p.m. on the calendar. It chose the toll-free 355-mile route, costed 12.7 gallons of fuel, and did the one subtraction that decides the evening: an eastbound drive across a zone line takes an hour more than the odometer suggests. ChatGPT wrote the better car kit and the worse trip. It put us on two toll roads without mentioning them, printed the mileage of a route it had not picked, and had us arriving 1 hour 53 minutes before we did.

The Short Answer

Gemini won on route and clock. ChatGPT won on the back seat. The split is narrow: both wrote about the dog, both answered in under a minute, and only one budgeted time for the stops. Only one of them understood that 3:10 p.m. in Chicago is 4:10 p.m. in Columbus.

That difference is worth more than it looks on a holiday Wednesday. On a flat 200-mile run inside a single zone you would take whichever plan read better, and the model-level comparison of the two is the page for that decision. Put a clock change and a four-year-old on the same route and the order sorts itself.

Thanksgiving Is a Clock Problem

Chicago runs on Central time. Columbus runs on Eastern. Between them sits Indiana, which is Eastern for most of its length and Central in the northwest corner around Gary and Hammond, so the line falls a few dozen miles into the drive instead of at a border you can point to on a paper map.

Eastbound that costs an hour. Westbound it hands one back, which is why the Sunday drive home is the easy half of the trip and the half nobody plans for.

ChatGPT never touched the arithmetic. It took the 3:10 departure, added 5 hours 20 minutes, and printed 8:30 p.m. as though the car were crossing between two clocks that read the same. It budgeted no stop time either, for a four-year-old and a dog, on the heaviest driving day of the year.

Gemini treated the zone change as part of the sum. Its arrival landed two minutes after mine, and those two minutes are a rounding difference in average speed rather than a missing step.

Thirty-five miles is not the interesting part. The interesting part is the number ChatGPT printed next to its own road numbers: 355 miles, for a route that runs 390.

It took the distance from the southern route and the road numbers from the northern one. A small error with a large tail, because that northern line runs through the snow belt east of Cleveland in the last week of November, and a family that believes it is on a 355-mile drive has budgeted neither the extra 37 minutes nor the turnpike.

Neither model quoted a toll amount, and I would not use either to. Rates belong to the authority, move with axle count and transponder, and are published by the turnpike rather than by a chatbot.

Two stops is the floor with a four-year-old and a dog, and 45 minutes is what they cost when nothing goes sideways. One efficient 30-minute stop looks better on paper and fails around hour five, which is exactly where this route puts Richmond.

The legs matter as much as the stops: 115 minutes, 118, then 95. Three stretches under two hours, which is the interval a four-year-old can usually hold. The first 30 miles out of Chicago and the ring around Indianapolis are where the schedule bleeds, and Gemini was the only one of the two that priced a stop after each of them.

ChatGPT's version was “leave at 5 a.m. to beat traffic,” which solves a problem this household does not have. The car cannot leave before school lets out.

The Dog and the Back Seat

Gemini was the only plan that treated the dog as a passenger: water at both stops, a 15-minute walk on grass, and a harness clipped to a seatbelt so she cannot come through the gap when a door opens at a rest area in the dark.

ChatGPT wrote the better kit, and it is not close. Eighteen lines, six of them for the kids: a lap tray, two blankets, a bag for the motion sickness the four-year-old gets after 90 minutes, headphones that work without a phone, a thermos, and a printed page with the last 40 minutes of directions for the moment the phone mount loses its argument with a pothole.

The list that actually earns its space is shorter. A two-gallon jug and a collapsible bowl, a six-foot leash, trash bags, a cooler with food the kids will eat so hunger does not become a fourth stop, and one blanket each. None of it needs to be bought the same day.

What Neither Plan Got Right

Fuel. Gemini's 12.7 gallons each way at 28 mpg is right for a loaded CR-V at highway speed, and it produces a conclusion: a 14-gallon tank gives 392 miles of range, so the car reaches Columbus with 37 miles left, and the fill belongs in Richmond rather than Lafayette. ChatGPT said “about a tank and a half,” which is true and useless.

The return. Westbound on Sunday, November 29, the same 355 miles costs an hour less on the clock. Leave at 8:00 a.m. Eastern with the same two stops and you are home at 1:13 p.m. Central. Gemini said before 7 or after 8, which reads the peak correctly. ChatGPT said 11 a.m., which drops a family into the worst four hours of the busiest return day of the year.

Weather. Neither checked a forecast. Both wrote as if I-70 would be dry from Richmond to Columbus, and in the last week of November that is a coin flip.

The Verdict

Winner: Gemini. Correct route, correct distance, correct tank math, and the only arrival time that survives a second look. Two minutes of error across 355 miles and one clock change is close to the floor for this kind of answer.

Where ChatGPT wins: the car and the kids. Take its packing list and its printed directions, throw away its route and its arrival time. The Thanksgiving week buying guide is where I would start if the question is which plan to pay for at all, and the kitchen version of this test covers the other half of the holiday.

The rule worth keeping: miles divided by 65, plus 45 minutes of stops, plus an hour if you are heading east across a zone line. Here that is 5:28 + 0:45 + 1:00, or 7 hours 13 minutes on the dash. Any plan that hands you a shorter number has not counted something.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to drive from Chicago to Columbus the day before Thanksgiving?

355 miles, about 5 hours 28 minutes of driving on I-65 and I-70. Two stops of 25 and 20 minutes bring the elapsed time to 6 hours 13 minutes, and because the drive crosses from Central into Eastern time the dashboard clock shows 7 hours 13 minutes. Leave Chicago at 3:10 p.m. and you pull in at 10:23 p.m. in Columbus.

Do you lose an hour driving from Chicago to Columbus?

Going east, yes. Chicago is on Central time and Columbus is on Eastern, and the change happens inside Indiana rather than at the Ohio line, because most of Indiana is Eastern while the northwest corner near Gary stays Central. The Sunday drive back returns the hour, so the same 355 miles shows one hour less on the dash than the eastbound run did.

What is the best time to leave Chicago on November 25?

Before 6 a.m. or after 7 p.m. if the schedule allows it. A 3:10 p.m. start with two stops lands at 10:23 p.m. Eastern, and the two stretches that cost the most are the first 30 miles out of the city and the ring around Indianapolis. A 5 a.m. departure beats traffic but not a 3 p.m. school pickup, which makes the afternoon the realistic version for a family.

Can ChatGPT or Gemini tell me what the tolls will cost?

Neither printed a figure in this test and neither should be trusted with one. Rates depend on the authority, the axle count and whether the car carries a transponder, and they change. What a model can do is tell you which route has tolls at all: the I-65 and I-70 run between Chicago and Columbus has none, while the northern route on I-90 and the Ohio Turnpike has two.
