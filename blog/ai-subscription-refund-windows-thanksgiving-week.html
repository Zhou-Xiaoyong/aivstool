Refunding an AI Subscription You Bought Thanksgiving Week: Three Vendors, Three Clocks

Five plans, $258 on one card, and three different answers to the only question anyone asks after they pay: if I change my mind, what comes back?

Five AI subscriptions bought between November 25 and November 30 charge $258. Change your mind on December 3 and $28 of that can come back. The other $230 cannot, and not because you missed a deadline: for a buyer in the United States, two of the three vendors publish no refund window at all.

That split is worth knowing before Black Friday, because the plans look identical on a pricing page. All three vendors let you cancel whenever you want. One of them returns money afterward.

The Seven Days Only Count If You Have Not Used It

OpenAI's window is narrower than the number sounds. The refund is discretionary, it applies to a subscription you have not used, it has to concern the current billing period, and it excludes accounts with repeat refunds, substantial use, an open payment dispute or confirmed abuse. The page also says plainly that an accidental purchase, forgetting to cancel, dissatisfaction or a plan change does not by itself qualify.

Then the sentence that costs people money: “Canceling a subscription by itself stops future renewals and does not establish refund eligibility.” Those are two separate actions. Cancel on December 3 and you have stopped the December charge. You have not asked for the $20 back, and canceling does not count as asking.

So treat the week as a reading window, not a trial. Open Plus on December 2 to see whether you like it and you have used the subscription. A request filed two days later is then a request for a used plan, and the honest answer is no.

The Twenty-Four Hour Rule Belongs to One Vendor

Anthropic's help center gives one timing instruction: cancel at least 24 hours before the next billing date to avoid the next charge, and the cancellation takes effect at the end of the current period, so access keeps running until then. For the November 26 monthly line the next charge lands December 26, which makes December 25 the last day to act before the charge lands.

Neither of the other two publishes that rule. GitHub's billing table says cancellation takes effect at the end of the cycle with no mention of a cutoff hour, and OpenAI's cancellation simply stops renewals. If you read the Claude page and then go hunting for the same rule at Copilot, you are hunting for something that was never written.

GitHub has a different quirk worth one line: the included AI credit allowance resets at 00:00 UTC on the first of each calendar month, whatever your billing date is. Sign up on November 25 and the first allowance covers six days, then resets on December 1.

The $200 Line Where Canceling Changes Nothing

Claude Pro is $20 a month or $200 prepaid for twelve months. The year saves $40 against twelve monthly charges, which is 16.7%, or $16.67 a month. It is also the only line in the basket where the decision is finished at checkout. Cancel on December 3 and you keep access until November 29, 2027, and nothing comes back.

That is the trade, not a trick. You prepaid for a year of access and you get a year of access. The mistake is buying the year in the same week you buy four other things, because that is the week your attention is spread thinnest. Our Black Friday payback math covers when the annual plan actually wins. Run the monthly for a month first and the question answers itself.

If You Bought It on Your Phone

Buying inside an iOS or Android app moves the refund decision to the store. Anthropic's page says App Store payments are handled by Apple and that support cannot access or refund them. OpenAI's says cancellations and refunds for Apple subscriptions have to be requested from Apple. The vendor's own seven-day courtesy does not travel with an App Store charge.

Practically: if you subscribed on your phone in a parking lot on Friday, open reportaproblem.apple.com or the Google Play order history first, and cancel in the same place you paid. Whether Plus earns its $20 is a separate question, and our free versus Plus breakdown answers that one.

What to Do Before Friday

  1. Buy the monthly tier if you are unsure. The whole exposure is one period: $8, $10 or $20. The prepaid year is the only line that cannot be undone.
  2. Write the refund deadline on the day you pay. Charge date plus seven, in your calendar, with the word “unused” next to it.
  3. Ask for the money and cancel as two requests. One stops the next charge. The other asks for the last one back, and only one of the three grants it without a legal right behind the request.
  4. Use the fourteen days if you are in the EEA or the UK. Two of the three publish it and both prorate by usage, so the earlier you ask the more comes back.
  5. Keep the prepaid year for a month you are paying attention. December 1 costs the same $200 as November 27 and comes with a clearer head, as the renewal calendar makes plain.

The Verdict

For a US buyer the refund question has one useful answer. ChatGPT is the only plan in this basket with a published window, it is seven days wide, and it is conditional on not using the thing. Claude and Copilot return nothing after the charge, so buy them monthly until you are sure, and buy the Claude year only when you can name what you will use it for in July.

None of this argues against the prepaid year. It argues against buying the prepaid year in the same week as everything else. Check the monthly against the free tier first, and read the Copilot tier breakdown before you assume the cheap plan is the safe one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I still get a refund on ChatGPT Plus after the seven days?

Not under the discretionary policy. OpenAI says there is no automatic prorated refund after seven days, and that forgetting to cancel does not qualify. Billing errors, unauthorized charges and documented access failures are handled as separate cases.

Does canceling Claude Pro refund the month I already paid for?

No. Cancellation stops the next charge and takes effect at the end of the current billing period, so you keep access until then. A refund is a separate request, available only where the Consumer Terms or local law provide one.

Is the $200 Claude Pro year refundable if I stop using it in January?

For a US buyer, no. The prepaid year is not refundable after the charge, and canceling in January leaves access running to the end of the prepaid period. EEA and UK buyers have fourteen days from purchase, prorated by usage.

Does GitHub Copilot refund unused days when I cancel?

No. GitHub's billing documentation states that access continues to the end of the current cycle and that there is no refund for unused time. The one prorated exception I found is organizational: if you are assigned a Copilot Business or Enterprise seat, your personal plan is canceled automatically with a prorated refund for the rest of its cycle.
