What Do You Say at Thanksgiving Dinner? Three Assistants, One 90-Second Toast

Dinner is at three tomorrow, the toast is yours, and it has to run under ninety seconds. The seven constraints that fixed all three drafts, and the one failure you cannot edit out at the table.

Two hundred words is eighty-six seconds. Say the toast after the plates are cleared, give the model the three names in your family instead of the roles, and put a hard word cap on it. Claude came back at 191 words with nothing invented. Gemini invented a cousin who was never at the table.

Dinner is at three tomorrow and the toast is yours. The problem is not that you have nothing to say. It is that the first draft reads like every other table this week, because the words that make a toast sound generated sit on one short list and all three models reach for it in the first sentence.

So I tested the thing you need tonight: one prompt, the same seven constraints, the free tier of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, run side by side on the evening of November 25, 2026. Then I read all three out loud at the pace of a room halfway through dinner, because that is where a sentence fails.

Ninety Seconds Is a Word Count, Not a Feeling

At 140 words a minute, the pace a table can follow without leaning in, ninety seconds is 210 words. I set the cap at 200 and left the last ten for the pause before the raise. Two minutes is 280 words, and that is where a toast turns into a speech and people start looking at the pie.

Placement matters as much as length. In most houses the toast lands after the main course is cleared and before dessert, when everyone is still at the table but nobody is holding a fork. If your family says grace before carving, you have two speeches rather than one, and the second has to be the shorter of the two. Do not stand up while the bird is still being dealt with.

There is a mechanical reason to write for the ear. The sentence that collapses at a table is almost always the long one with two commas in the middle, the kind a model writes when it is trying to sound considered. Reading it out loud finds it in seconds. Scanning a screen never does.

The Seven Constraints That Did the Work

A bare “write me a Thanksgiving toast” produces the same greeting card three times. What I sent was a spec:

“Write a 90-second Thanksgiving toast I will read out loud tomorrow. Under 200 words. Name my mother Diane, my brother Marcus, and my grandmother Ruth, who died in March. One gentle joke at Marcus’s expense about the deep fryer. Mention the burnt rolls. Do not use the words blessed, grateful, gratitude, thankful, journey, abundance or circle. End on a toast line of six words or fewer. Do not invent any fact about my family that I have not given you.”

Each line has a job. The cap controls the clock. The three names stop the model from spending four sentences on “to the family.” The death gives the draft one real note of weight, and one is right. The banned list removes the vocabulary of a fundraising email. The six-word close forces an ending instead of a fade. The last line is the one that only one of the three held to, and it is the finding of the test.

The Failure That Is Not a Cliché

Ten banned words across three drafts is a copy-editing problem. A cousin named Sarah is not.

Gemini’s draft ran 344 words and brought in “cousin Sarah, who drove eleven hours with the green bean casserole.” There is no Sarah in my family, and nobody drove eleven hours. On a screen that is a harmless flourish. At a table of eleven people who know exactly who came and what they brought, it is the one mistake you cannot repair while your mouth is open. Nobody corrects a cliché out loud. Everybody notices a person who was not there.

ChatGPT made the same move at lower volume: Diane “up since six,” and Ruth’s “index card” for the dressing. Neither is true, and both sound like they must be, which is exactly what makes them risky. Claude reached for the same warmth and put it in a joke instead of a fact, which is the difference between a line that can be wrong and a line that can only be flat.

If You Are Reading This at 2 p.m. Tomorrow

Skip the drafts. Four moves in this order work cold. Open with one thing that actually happened this year, in a sentence with a month in it. Name one person at the table and something specific they did, not how you feel about them. Name one person who is not at the table, once, and do not explain it. Then stop: “To all of us.” The room fills the rest.

If someone is already looking at you and the page is blank, the only lever left is length. Two sentences beat four. A short toast that lands badly is thirty seconds; a long one that lands badly becomes the thing people bring up at Christmas.

The Verdict

Claude won on the least glamorous habit available: it stayed inside the brief. 191 words, all three names, one banned word, a joke that fit on a line, and no relative who does not exist. Four minutes from paste to something I would say out loud.

ChatGPT is the one to open if you want to see the shape before you want the voice. It found the structure fastest and then spent it: 268 words against a 200-word cap, two invented details, and a joke that needed two sentences to land. Trimmed to the brief it is a good toast, and the trimming is yours at ten o’clock at night.

Gemini wrote the most complete speech and the least usable toast. 344 words, one of three names, six banned words, a cousin who does not exist, and a closing line that took nineteen words to arrive. As raw material to raid for one phrase it is fine. As something to read at three o’clock it is a liability.

The rule that survives is not about models. An exact word count, real names, and a banned-word list get a usable draft out of any of the three. Frequently Asked Questions

How many words is a 90-second Thanksgiving toast?

About 210 at 140 words a minute, which is the pace a room follows without effort. Write to 200 and you have ten words of slack for the pause before the raise. Two minutes is 280 words, and past that most tables stop listening even if they keep smiling.

What should a Thanksgiving toast actually say?

Four moves: one thing that happened this year with a month attached, one person at the table and what they did, one person who is not there, then a short line to drink to. That is ninety seconds and it beats any speech built on gratitude in the abstract, which is the register every model defaults to.

Should I use AI to write a Thanksgiving toast?

For structure and for a first pass, yes. For facts about your family, no. Three drafts in this test contained three invented details, including a cousin who does not exist, and a wrong detail at a table of people who were there is worse than a dull line. Give the model the names and the true details, then check every clause against memory before you say it.

When in the meal should the toast happen?

After the main course is cleared and before dessert, while everyone is still seated but nobody is holding a fork. If your family says grace before carving, keep that one to fifteen seconds and save the real toast for later, because the person carving is who the table is waiting on.

Do I need a paid plan to write one toast?

No. All three drafts in this test came from free tiers, and one toast fits well inside the free daily message limit. Paying $20 buys longer sessions and stronger models, which pays off across a season of writing rather than a single evening.
