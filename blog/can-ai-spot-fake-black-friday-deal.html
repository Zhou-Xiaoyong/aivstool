Can AI Spot a Fake Black Friday Deal? Six Tickets, Three Assistants, Six Wrong Prices

A $2,563.96 cart, sixteen list prices, and six that were wrong. Which assistant read the pages, which one invented last November, and the ninety-second check that beats all three.

More than a third of what comes back is guesswork, delivered in the same flat tone as the correct parts. Across sixteen list prices for six Black Friday tickets, the three assistants got six wrong, and ChatGPT added five “last Black Friday” numbers with no source behind any of them. The figure that decides whether a discount is real, the street price, was in almost none of the answers.

A discount measured against list price says nothing about what you would pay in March. That gap decides whether Thursday night is worth the trip, and none of the three could see it, because seeing it means reading a price chart rather than recalling a price.

I ran the test on November 24, 2026, three days before Black Friday, on the free tier of each tool, memory off, one message per item, ninety seconds on the clock per item. Every number below was checked against the maker's own product page after the test.

Six Tickets, One Prompt, Ninety Seconds

The cart was an ordinary one. Sony WH-1000XM5 headphones at $278.00, a Dyson V15 Detect at $499.99, a KitchenAid Artisan five-quart at $329.99, an 11-inch iPad at $279.00, an LG 55-inch C4 OLED at $996.99 and a Ninja CREAMi at $179.99. Together $2,563.96 against $3,678.95 of list, which is the $1,114.99 the tags were advertising.

Every ticket sits between 20.1% and 33.5% off list, which is the range real cuts live in and also the range a marked-up list price buys for free. So I did not ask whether the thing is worth owning. I asked what the real list price is, what it sold for last November, and where that number came from.

Where the Cut Is Real and Where It Is Arithmetic

Two of the six were arithmetic dressed as a sale. The iPad at 20.1% off list is roughly $20 under its usual street price, and the CREAMi at 21.7% off is about $10 under. Neither is a bad purchase. Both are a bad reason to leave the house at five in the morning.

The other four hold up: 30.5% for the Sony, 33.3% for the Dyson, 26.7% for the KitchenAid, 33.5% for the LG. Each is a generation old and the list price never came down, which is the shape of a real Black Friday cut: an old model, an untouched list price, and a ticket that finally meets the street.

What Each Assistant Actually Said

ChatGPT answered all six in nine seconds and got three list prices wrong: $649.99 for the Dyson, $1,299.99 for the LG, $199.99 for the CREAMi. Then it told me what each item sold for last Black Friday, five times, with no source I could check. Recalled prices failing at the exact moment you need them is the same failure our $191.10 grocery cart test found on a smaller scale.

Gemini was the only one that said it did not know. It declined to price the KitchenAid and the CREAMi outright, which is correct behavior and still leaves you shopping without a number. On the four it did price it went two for four: $349.99 for the Sony, $799.99 for the Dyson, correct on the iPad and the LG. Its buy-or-wait calls came from a rule, not from data. Wait on four, buy on two.

Perplexity went and read pages. Four citations, a price-history service named for two items, five of six list prices right, the LG missed by $100. It also refused to state last November's price and gave the current street price with the page it came from instead, which costs it a number and earns it the only trust in the test.

Then it hit the ceiling. Three Pro searches a day on the free tier, and items four, five and six returned paler answers: no citations, no prices I could verify without opening a tab myself. That is a capacity limit, not a quality one, and it is the argument for spending the $20 on the plan instead of the gadget.

Black Friday 2026 Opens Thursday Night

The sales start on Thanksgiving evening, November 26, and the hardware and software pricing runs through Cyber Monday on November 30. Four of the six tickets above are already live at the price I pasted on November 24. The tag you see at 8 p.m. on Thursday is usually the tag that survives the weekend.

Two things do move on Friday: bundles that appear on no price page today, and stock on the two oldest models. Neither changes a street price, which is the only number that answers the question.

On the AI subscriptions themselves, the one discount that is real this week is the annual prepay, and it does not improve on Friday. We priced that out in the Black Friday plan math, and the arithmetic there does not change.

The Ninety-Second Check to Run Yourself

Open the price history before the chatbot. For anything sold on Amazon that means CamelCamelCamel or Keepa, and the chart answers both questions: the lowest price in the past twelve months, and whether the “list” price has been the same number all year. For everything else, the maker's product page carries the list price, with the model year beside it.

Read the ticket against that low. Below the twelve-month low is a buy. Within 10% of it is a buy if you need the thing this week. Above it, the discount is a marketing budget. Then hand the item to an assistant for the part it is good at: whether the model is worth owning.

If You Already Bought

If a numbered tag and a helpful assistant talked you into a purchase, the return window is the last lever, and many retailers extend it across the holidays. Check the date printed on the receipt before the box is opened. Unopened is a refund; opened is often a restocking fee, which can eat the discount you thought you got.

The Verdict

Perplexity won on one habit: it opened pages. Five list prices right out of six, four citations, and the honesty to admit it could not see last November. Its limit is the three Pro searches, so run the cart in one sitting and spend them on the three items you will actually pay for.

ChatGPT is the tool for after the cart, when the question is whether the Dyson is worth owning. Its list prices were close enough to be useful; its last-Black-Friday numbers were not usable at all.

Gemini declined twice, which beats ChatGPT on behavior and still loses on the job. Two of its four numbers were wrong, and its wait-on-everything rule would have cost you the two genuine discounts in the cart. On a price question, a refusal plus a wrong number is the same as a wrong number.

None of the three can read a price chart. That is the finding, and it is worth ninety seconds to open one yourself. If the question is whether the free tier gets you through Friday at all, the free-tier test has the pass and fail numbers, and whether Plus is worth it is the honest accounting for the rest of the year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI tell me if a Black Friday price is real?

Partly, and less than it sounds. All three assistants can state a list price, and six of the sixteen list prices I collected were wrong, which is enough to flip a verdict either way. None of them can see a twelve-month price chart, so none can tell you whether the ticket beats the street price. Use the assistant for whether the product is worth owning and a price tracker for whether the price is real.

How do I check whether a Black Friday deal is a real discount?

Run four numbers in ninety seconds: the model year, the maker's list price, the lowest price in the past twelve months, and the ticket. CamelCamelCamel or Keepa covers anything sold on Amazon; the maker's product page covers everything else. A ticket below the twelve-month low is a buy, within 10% of it is a buy if you need it now, and above it the discount is advertising.

Is Cyber Monday better than Black Friday for AI tools?

Not for the subscriptions. The annual prepay is the only real discount in this category, it runs at the same terms through Monday, and the three plans I priced on November 24 sit at the same list prices our earlier checks recorded in November. Cyber Monday matters for hardware bundles, not for the $20 assistant plans.

Will ChatGPT Plus or Perplexity Pro be cheaper on Black Friday?

As of November 24, 2026, no. ChatGPT Plus is $20 a month with no annual price on its pricing page, Google AI Pro is $19.99 a month, and Perplexity Pro is $20 a month or $200 a year. The annual prepay is the only line that moves, and it is worth roughly two months free.

I already bought something at a fake discount. What can I do?

Work the return window and leave the box sealed. An unopened return is usually a refund, while an opened one can carry a restocking fee that cancels the saving. If the same item drops further on Friday, ask for the difference rather than shipping it back: no retailer is obliged to adjust, and the ones that do usually want the receipt and the request within a week or two.
