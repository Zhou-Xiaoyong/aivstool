ChatGPT vs Perplexity: A $191.10 Thanksgiving Cart for 11

Same brief, same store, one receipt. Perplexity turned 11 guests into sticks, cups and bag counts. ChatGPT left nine lines without an amount and put four pantry items back on the list.

Eleven people ate for $191.10, and $35.97 of that was wine.

The receipt has 24 lines. Turkey took $36.22 of it, so the two things a Thanksgiving cart is really about came to $72.19 and everything else held at $118.91: potatoes, cream, cranberries, rolls, a disposable pan, coffee nobody planned for.

One of those lists could be shopped as written. The other could not, and that is the whole finding.

On the morning of Saturday, November 7, 2026, I handed the same brief to ChatGPT and to Perplexity, walked into one supermarket with both answers on my phone, and kept the receipt. We ate on Sunday the 8th because half the family flies to Ohio on the 20th. Your trip is Tuesday the 24th and none of the arithmetic below changes except the date printed at the top.

Bottom line: shop from Perplexity's list. It turned 11 guests into pounds, sticks, cups and bag counts, and every conversion survived the register. ChatGPT wrote the better gravy and spotted the pan, and still produced the worse cart in three ways: nine of its 26 lines named an ingredient with no amount, and four of its lines were things the brief said were already in the pantry. Its one advantage is real too. It never quoted a price, and the one price Perplexity quoted cost me $6.00 more than advertised.

The Short Answer

Ask for pack sizes, not ingredients. "Butter" is not a purchase. Two 1-pound boxes is a purchase, and it is the difference between a list you follow and a list you interpret while holding a cart in the dairy aisle.

Perplexity returned 31 lines with an amount on every one. ChatGPT returned 26 with amounts on 17. The cart that came out of the first one rang up $191.10 against a $200 ceiling, which leaves $8.90 for the second trip nobody plans for.

If you are still deciding what software to pay for this week, that is a different page: the Thanksgiving week buying guide prices the tools, and the annual plan math covers what the 27th actually cuts. This one is about the register.

What 11 Guests Convert To

The brief was not a blank prompt. It named 11 guests (8 adults, 3 kids), the nine items on the menu, one store, a $200 ceiling, and four things already in the cupboard: flour, sugar, cinnamon, vanilla. That last clause is the one both models had to honor and only one did.

The butter line is where most lists break. ChatGPT wrote "2 cups," which is 4 sticks, which is one pound, which is not enough for two pie crusts, mashed potatoes for 11, a rub on an 18-pound bird, and butter for the rolls. Six sticks was the real number, and six sticks is not a thing a store sells, so the answer is two 1-pound boxes and two sticks in the door shelf by Sunday.

Cream has the same shape. Five cups needed, and the dairy case sells quarts and pints, so a quart plus a pint is 6 cups and one cup left over for Monday's coffee. Nobody wants to discover that at 2 p.m. on Thanksgiving with the pies already in the oven.

Bakery and pantry is the biggest bucket at $41.66, and it is the one almost nobody budgets for. Stuffing cubes, stock, cranberries, pumpkin, brown sugar, rolls: none of it looks like Thanksgiving dinner on its own, and all of it is on the receipt.

Per person the cart ran $17.37 all in, or $12.15 with the wine and the non-food taken out. For 11 people eating turkey, two starches, two vegetables, two pies and a pot of coffee, that is the number to hold against whatever a restaurant quote would be.

Nine Blanks in ChatGPT's List

Butter. Heavy cream. Chicken stock. Cranberries. Stuffing. Green beans. Sweet potatoes. Wine. Foil. Nine lines, an ingredient name on each, no amount on any.

A list like that is not useless. It is a menu with the shopping left as homework, and the homework happens in an aisle at 9 a.m. with a cart and a phone and 40 minutes. "Sweet potatoes" with no count is a guess between four and eight, and the guess costs either a second trip or a bowl of orange mush nobody eats.

The pantry clause failed too. ChatGPT kept flour, sugar, vanilla and cinnamon on the list after being told all four were already in the cupboard. Buying them would have added $13.76 to a cart that was already within $8.90 of the ceiling, and following that list as written puts the trip at $204.86, which is $4.86 over.

ChatGPT's make-ahead gravy, built on a roux and finished with the bird's drippings on Thursday morning, is the one instruction from either model I kept. It also caught that a disposable pan is cheaper than scrubbing a roasting pan at 9 p.m., though it never said to buy one.

The One Price Perplexity Got Wrong

Green beans. Its list said "$0.99 a pound in this week's ad," and it cited the store's own flyer for it. The shelf said $2.49. Four pounds went from a promised $3.96 to $9.96 at the register, a $6.00 miss on four pounds of beans.

That is not a reason to throw the list out. It is a reason to treat a cited price as a range. A weekly ad is true for six days and the page stays up longer. Perplexity's quantity work was the part that held; its price work was the part that did not.

Where it earned its keep was the empty shelf. The 24-count rolls were gone and only a 36-count bag was left, and the stuffing cubes were down to one brand. Both gaps were already covered: it had written "any count over 22 works" next to the rolls and "any plain 14-oz cube bag" next to the stuffing, so neither one cost me a decision in the aisle. ChatGPT was never asked a follow-up, so it never had the chance to offer a swap, and that is a caveat on this comparison rather than a point against it.

What Neither of Them Listed

Coffee and foil. $17.48 of the cart, and neither model put either one on a list for a household cooking a 5-hour meal for 11 people.

That is the gap between a recipe and a kitchen. Two pounds of coffee is not a Thanksgiving ingredient until 7 p.m. on Thursday when four adults are still at the table and the pot is empty. Foil is not an ingredient until the pan has to be covered at the exact moment the bird comes out.

The Verdict

Winner: Perplexity, for the cart. 31 lines, an amount on every one, two substitutions already written into it, and a total that landed $8.90 under the ceiling. The one price it asserted was $6.00 light, which is the smallest miss on either answer and the easiest one to check against a shelf tag.

Take ChatGPT's gravy. The roux-and-drippings method is better than Perplexity's "thicken with cornstarch," and it is the only line from either answer that changed how Thursday went.

The rule worth keeping: convert people into pounds, sticks, cups and bag counts before you leave the house, then round up to the pack size the store actually sells. Eleven guests at 1.5 lb is 16.5 lb, the case had an 18.2-lb bird, and the 1.7 lb difference is a sandwich on Friday, not waste.

Where this stops applying: at 25 guests the per-person multipliers hold but the pack sizes stop working, and a warehouse club beats any supermarket list on the turkey and the butter alone. For four people, halve the bird and skip the second box of butter. The arithmetic is the same either way. Only the rounding changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much turkey do I need for 11 people?

Sixteen and a half pounds at 1.5 lb a person, so buy an 18-lb bird. The extra 1.7 lb is Friday's sandwiches, not waste. If the group is light on seconds, 1.25 lb a person covers 11 people at 14 lb.

Can ChatGPT write a grocery list with quantities?

Yes, if you ask for pack sizes rather than ingredients. In this test it returned 26 lines with an amount on 17, and the nine blanks were the ones that cost time in the aisle. Add three instructions to the brief: give every line a number, convert to the pack size the store sells, and skip anything listed as already in the pantry.

Is Perplexity better than ChatGPT for grocery prices?

For quantities, yes. For prices, treat what it cites as a range. It quoted green beans at $0.99 a pound from the store's weekly ad and the shelf read $2.49, which came to $6.00 more at the register on a $4 item. Check the two or three items that carry the budget, which for Thanksgiving are the bird, the wine and the butter.

How much does Thanksgiving dinner cost per person?

$17.37 for 11 people in November 2026, or $12.15 with the wine and the non-food taken out. That covers turkey, mashed potatoes, green beans, stuffing, sweet potatoes, cranberry sauce, two pies, rolls and coffee, bought at one supermarket with no coupons and no membership discount.
