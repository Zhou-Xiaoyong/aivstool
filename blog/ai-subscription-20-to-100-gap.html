The $80 Gap: AI Plans Jump From $20 to $100, With Nothing in Between

One month at the top tier costs five months of Plus. Two $20 plans cost $39.99 and cover the same weekend for $10 a day. The arithmetic, and what both assistants got wrong.

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini each sell a $20 plan and a $100 plan. None of them sells anything in between, and Thanksgiving week is when that hole starts costing money.

The next rung up sits at exactly five times the $20 plan at all three vendors, so the gap is $80 a month whether you are buying more messages, more storage or longer agent runs. A four-day project with a Sunday deadline is the week where a normal plan stops being enough, and there is no $40 option to step onto.

I wrote the three ladders out on November 20, 2026, six days before Thanksgiving, then handed the same brief to ChatGPT Plus and Claude Pro to see which one could plan around the hole without inventing a middle tier. Prices below are the ones each vendor lists on its US pricing page as of November 2026. Nobody had a holiday price on November 18 either: OpenAI and Anthropic both carried their regular numbers that day, and Claude's annual option is available in July as readily as in November.

Read the last column. Five times is not a pricing accident, it is what a capacity tier looks like. The $100 plans sell throughput: more messages, longer runs, higher ceilings. That is why the jump fits so badly for someone who needs a little more than $20 and nothing like five times more.

Anthropic's ladder is the steepest at the bottom. There is no paid tier under Pro at all, so a Claude user who wants to spend $10 has nowhere to go. OpenAI sells Go at $8 and Google starts at $19.99, which gives two of the three vendors a cheap rung. None of them has a middle one.

What One $100 Month Actually Costs

One month at the top tier costs the same as five months of Plus at $20. Put against a four-day deadline, that is $25 a day. A project that matters can be worth $25 a day, and the number is worth saying out loud before you click upgrade.

Budget the full $100 for the month you move up, not the $80 difference. None of the three pricing pages states that unused days on the cheaper plan are credited when you upgrade, and a budget built on a credit that never arrives is how a $20 subscription turns into a $100 one.

Then comes the forgetting problem, which is the expensive half. A $100 tier left running for a year is $1,200 against the $240 you were paying, a drift of $960 that happens with no decision after the first one. The upgrade is not the risk. The renewal eleven months later is.

The $39.99 Route Nobody Prices

Two $20 plans at two vendors cost $39.99 a month and give you two accounts, two sets of limits and two model families. For a project that splits by task, that is the middle rung nobody built: $10 a day across the four-day weekend, against $25 a day for a single $100 upgrade.

Where the Second Plan Stops Working

Splitting by vendor only works when the work can move. A cookbook can: text in one tool, page-layout notes in another, image prompts in whichever one renders them. An agent run cannot. If the project is one pipeline inside a single account, a second account elsewhere does nothing about the cap you hit, because the cap belongs to the account doing the running.

That is the case for $100, and it is narrower than the tier names suggest. Long video renders, agent sessions that hold one context for hours, batch jobs across a few hundred files: those do not split. A menu, a seating chart, forty pages of recipes and a week of social posts do.

The Team Seat Is Not the Middle Either

Business seats look like the $40 answer and are not one. ChatGPT Team bills $25 a seat with a two-seat minimum, so the smallest order is $50 a month, and each seat is a separate login rather than a second set of limits on your own account. Claude Team has a five-seat floor, $125 a month, which is more than the $100 tier it was supposed to undercut. Two individual plans at $40 keep their own billing, their own chat history and no administrator.

If You Buy the $100 Tier, Book the Exit First

Upgrade on a Friday and cancel on day 28. You keep the plan until the paid month ends, which is the point of buying 30 days rather than a subscription. Put the cancellation on the same calendar as the deadline, because a reminder set for after the holidays lands in January with a second $100 charge behind it.

Do not fold an annual plan into that decision. Claude Pro at $200 for twelve months is $40 off twelve monthly payments, and it is the one purchase here you cannot undo inside a week, as the refund clocks show.

Two Checks Before You Upgrade

Confirm the renewal date first. Moving up one day before a renewal wastes nothing; moving up three weeks into a paid month wastes most of it, and no vendor promises to credit those days back. Then work out whether the ceiling you hit is per account or per feature, because only the first one is a reason to change plans. Ten minutes of that is the difference between a $100 project and a $1,200 habit.

What I Would Do With Six Days Left

Four days, a project that splits, a tight month: two $20 plans. ChatGPT Plus for structure and copy, Google AI Pro at $19.99 for long documents and the 2 TB that comes with it, $39.99 in total. The $20 tier comparison settles which one takes the text half before you pay for both.

One pipeline, one account, a hard Thursday: the $100 tier for one month. Cost it at $100, cancel on day 28, and leave the annual question until February.

Already paying $20 and hitting limits twice a week all year: neither. That is a habit, not a deadline, and a habit is what annual billing is for. The year-up-front arithmetic settles it in one table, and what the free tier stops doing tells you whether the $20 is doing any work at all.

The Verdict

For a Thanksgiving-week project, two $20 plans beat one $100 plan: $39.99 against $100, two sets of limits instead of one, and nothing left running in December. The exception is a single pipeline that cannot leave its account, where the $100 month is the only lever and the day-28 cancellation is what keeps it a $100 decision instead of a $1,200 one.

The boundaries are real. Claude has no paid plan under Pro, so a Claude user who wants to spend $10 has no option at Anthropic. OpenAI's $8 Go plan is a different product with ads rather than a discounted Plus. Google's cheap rung is the $19.99 plan itself, not a stripped version of it. None of the three credits unused days when you move up, which is why an upgrade belongs the day before a renewal and not in the middle of a paid month. Where your renewal date falls decides that, and what each plan costs per usable hour is the check on whether a second plan is buying hours you will actually use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there an AI plan between $20 and $100 a month?

Not at the three big vendors. ChatGPT goes $8, $20, then Pro from $100. Claude goes free, $20, then Max at $100. Google goes $19.99 then Ultra at $100. The only things that fit in the gap are a second vendor's $20 plan at $39.99 for two, or a business seat, and ChatGPT Team starts at $50 for two seats while Claude Team starts at $125 for five.

Does upgrading for one month cost $100 or the $80 difference?

Budget $100. The $100 tier replaces the $20 one for that month rather than adding to it, and none of the three pricing pages states that unused days on the cheaper plan are credited when you move up. Planning on an $80 charge and getting a $100 one is the most common way this decision goes wrong.

Can two $20 plans replace one $100 plan?

Only when the work can move between vendors. Text, research, menus, seating charts and a week of social posts split cleanly, and two accounts at $39.99 beat one $100 tier on cost and on flexibility. A single agent session, a long video render or a batch job that has to stay inside one account does not split, because the cap belongs to the account doing the running.

Do AI subscriptions go on sale for Black Friday?

On November 18, 2026, neither OpenAI nor Anthropic listed a holiday price on its US pricing page, and the Google plan is still the $19.99 it is every other month. The one discount that looks seasonal is Claude Pro at $200 for twelve months instead of $240, and annual billing is available in any month. Waiting until November 27 buys the same price you can get today.

What happens if I forget to cancel a $100 upgrade?

It renews at $100 a month. A year at the top tier is $1,200 against $240 for the $20 plan, so a forgotten upgrade costs $960 more than the subscription you meant to keep. Cancel on day 28, inside the month you paid for, and the total stays at $100.
