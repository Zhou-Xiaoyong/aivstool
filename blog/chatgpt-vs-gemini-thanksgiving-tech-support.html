ChatGPT vs Gemini for Thanksgiving Tech Support: Four Family Problems, Scored

One laptop, one phone, one printer and one scam text, handed over between Wednesday night and Friday morning. 4 of 4 fixes against 3 of 4, 55 minutes against 110, and one answer that would have cost a card number.

Four dead devices, 165 minutes of stopwatch time, and one sentence that would have cost my mother a card number.

The sentence came from Gemini. Everything else it did that week beat ChatGPT, which is why the split below matters more than the scoreline.

I got to my mother's house outside Erie, Pennsylvania at 6:40 p.m. on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. By 7:05 the four problems were on the back of an envelope in her handwriting: a laptop that takes forever, a phone that says it is full, a printer that stopped printing, and a text asking for $1.99. Her guest list for the 26th was already on the fridge, and those four devices get handed across that table in two weeks.

The printer had to work by Friday the 13th, because the return label inside it was for a package that had to reach the shipping counter before noon. Both assistants got the same four screenshots and the same brief, one pass each, no follow-ups. Every step was timed and applied in the order given, including the ones that went nowhere.

Bottom line: Gemini fixed four of four on its first answer in 55 minutes. ChatGPT fixed three in 110, and 65 of those went to a printer that never printed. The scam-text advice is the part I would not put near a parent: Gemini told her to open the link on a computer instead of the phone. ChatGPT wrote two sentences that ended the conversation. Use Gemini on the settings menus. Hand ChatGPT the sentence you say out loud.

The Short Answer

Split by what the answer looks like. If the fix lives in a settings screen, Gemini was faster every time: it read the screenshot, named the toggle, skipped the interview. If the fix is a sentence you say to a nervous 71-year-old, ChatGPT wrote the better one and Gemini wrote a bad one.

Two numbers hold the comparison. First-answer fixes: 4 of 4 against 3 of 4. Minutes to four working devices: 55 against 110.

If the question is which software to pay for over the break, the Thanksgiving week buying guide prices the plans per usable hour.

One: The Laptop, and a Troubleshooter That No Longer Ships

The 2016 Inspiron took 6 minutes 40 seconds from power button to a usable desktop, and 48 seconds for the first Chrome tab to paint. Eight startup entries, four of them updaters.

ChatGPT opened by asking which Windows build the machine was on, which was fair and cost one turn. Then it named a path I wrote down and followed: Settings, System, Troubleshoot, Other troubleshooters, Hardware and Devices. That troubleshooter does not exist in Windows 11. I spent four minutes hunting a button Microsoft removed.

Gemini opened with the disk: a 5400-rpm drive is the bottleneck, not the 8 GB of RAM, and the fix is a clone to a SATA solid-state drive. It also put a $29.99-a-year tune-up app first, which I did not install.

The clone took 42 minutes of waiting and 15 of my hands, through a USB-to-SATA adapter. Boot went to 22 seconds, first tab to 9. The drive cost $58.99, or 13.1% of the $449 laptop I would otherwise have bought.

Two: The Phone, at 96.4 GB in 128

Four thousand two hundred photos at 54.6 GB, 380 videos at 41.8 GB, 96.4 GB of library on a 128 GB phone. For a month it had coped by refusing new pictures at the worst possible moment: a grandchild's birthday.

The iCloud arithmetic decides it. Free is 5 GB. The $0.99 tier is 50 GB, and 96.4 does not fit in 50, so it is not the cheaper option, it is wrong by 46.4 GB. The 200 GB tier at $2.99 leaves 103.6 GB of headroom.

ChatGPT reached the same tier and the Optimize iPhone Storage toggle, but after duplicate cleanup, a computer-based import and a question about whether she had a Mac. Gemini led with the toggle, then the tier, then the number. Same answer, three paragraphs earlier.

Three: The Printer, and the 65 Minutes

The Brother HL-L2350DW went offline on November 20, the afternoon a new router went in. Windows 11 said Offline, three documents sat in the queue, and one was a return label with a noon deadline.

ChatGPT diagnosed a driver. Uninstall, reboot, download, reinstall: 24 minutes, still offline. Then a registry key for 0x0000011b, the failure mode for a printer shared off another PC, not for one with its own address. Twelve more minutes on a machine that never shared anything. Then it suggested Brother support. Sixty-five minutes, no page.

Gemini asked for one thing: the network configuration page, printed from the printer’s own panel. The printer had come back on the new router at 192.168.1.23. Windows was still pointing at 192.168.1.14, the address the old router had leased it. New TCP/IP port, clear the queue, print. Eighteen minutes, and the label came out.

Nothing about the driver had changed, which is why the driver answer could not work. A router swap reassigns addresses.

Four: The Text, Where Gemini Lost the Room

The screenshot read: USPS, your parcel is on hold, pay $1.99 to reschedule, at a domain that was not usps.com. USPS does not collect redelivery fees through a text link. The whole test takes four seconds.

ChatGPT named it smishing, said not to tap it, said to forward it to 7726 and delete, then did the thing that mattered: two sentences my mother could repeat to her sister, who got the same text. I used them.

Gemini also called it likely fraudulent, then added that if she wanted to be sure she could open the link on a computer rather than the phone. That is the scam, relocated: a lookalike page in Chrome on a desktop asks for a card number as happily as one in Safari.

Five minutes each. One of those answers is why the scoreline and the recommendation are not the same thing.

The Order to Do Them In

The order matters more than the tool. This is the sequence for the 25th through the 27th, in the minutes the stopwatch produced.

Wednesday night, the laptop first. The clone runs unattended for 42 minutes, so start it and walk away.

While it clones, the phone. Twelve minutes, no computer needed, and the job most likely to become an argument if it waits until Friday.

Thursday morning, the text. Five minutes, before the bird goes in the oven. A frightened parent who waits until after dinner has usually forwarded it to three relatives, and one of them taps.

Friday morning, the printer. Eighteen minutes if you suspect the port, 65 if you start with the driver. It has to be done before the shipping counter closes, so it cannot be the job you save for Sunday.

Total: 97 minutes door to door, 55 of them with my hands on a keyboard.

The Verdict

Winner: Gemini, four problems to three. The margin is the printer, 65 minutes against 18 and one device that never worked. Add the laptop, where it skipped a menu path that no longer exists, and the pattern holds: for anything with a screenshot and a settings screen, it is the faster first answer.

Do not hand Gemini the scam text. The answer I actually used came from ChatGPT, and “open it on a computer instead” is the worst line either model produced that week. If the job is talking a relative out of something, ChatGPT wrote the better script.

The rule worth keeping: photograph the screen, state the device and the model year, and ask for the steps in order plus a dollar figure. Both models improved the moment the brief said “assume I can only try each step once.” That clause is why the timings mean anything.

Where this stops applying: if the machine will not boot at all, the answer is a screwdriver, not a chatbot. If the household has one device and no deadline, the free tiers are enough. The paid tiers earn their $20 at four devices and a Friday deadline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can ChatGPT or Gemini fix a printer that went offline after a router change?

Only if you ask about the port. Gemini asked for the network configuration page, found the printer at 192.168.1.23, and saw Windows pointing at 192.168.1.14 from the old router lease. That took 18 minutes. ChatGPT replaced the driver instead and never printed. If a printer dies the day a router is installed, the address is the first suspect and the driver is not.

Is a USPS redelivery text asking for $1.99 a scam?

Yes, if the link does not go to usps.com. USPS does not collect redelivery fees through a text link and does not text unsolicited payment requests. Do not tap it, forward it to 7726 so the carrier can block the number, then delete it. Opening the link on a computer instead of a phone does not make it safer.

How long does an SSD upgrade take on an old laptop, and is it worth it?

About 42 minutes to clone the drive and 15 to swap it. On the 2016 Inspiron here, boot went from 6 minutes 40 seconds to 22 seconds and the first browser tab from 48 seconds to 9. A 1 TB SATA drive cost $58.99 against $449 for a comparable new laptop.

Which iCloud plan do I need for 4,200 photos?

The 200 GB tier at $2.99 a month, for a 96.4 GB library. The $0.99 tier is 50 GB and is 46.4 GB short, so it fails on the first backup. Turn on Optimize iPhone Storage first, then pick the tier that leaves room to grow: 200 GB leaves 103.6 GB of headroom.
