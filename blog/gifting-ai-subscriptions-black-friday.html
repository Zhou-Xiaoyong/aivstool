Black Friday AI Gifts 2026: Five Routes, and Whose Card Gets Charged Later

A $25 ChatGPT card is one month of Plus plus $5. The $20 of gift credits sold inside ChatGPT buys no subscription at all. Five routes, priced, with the clock on each.

A $25 ChatGPT gift card buys one month of Plus and leaves $5 in the account. The $20 of gift credits you can buy from inside ChatGPT buys no subscription at all.

Those are two OpenAI products with the same purpose printed on the label. I read OpenAI's gifting help page, Anthropic's gift article and the US retail listings for the ChatGPT cards on November 19, 2026, eight days before Black Friday. Gifting an AI subscription is a different product from buying one yourself, and most of the difference hides in a line nobody reads at checkout.

The five routes below all exist, though one of them is closed to new buyers. They differ on one question: after the money you put in is gone, whose card gets charged?

A gift that turns into a subscription in someone else's name keeps charging them. A gift that lands as a balance runs out and stops. The price on the shelf does not tell you which one you picked up.

The Card: $100 Buys Five Months of Plus

OpenAI's digital cards come in four values, sold in the US through retailers including Best Buy. The code is redeemed at chatgpt.com/redeem and the money lands in the recipient's ChatGPT balance. The retail listing says that balance covers a new subscription, a renewal and Codex credits, which makes the card the only OpenAI product that can pay for Plus itself.

Plus has no annual price on OpenAI's US pricing page, so a card is measured in months at $20 and nothing else. The $200 card is ten months of Plus, the closest thing to an annual plan OpenAI sells. When the tenth month ends, the plan stops. No renewal fires unless the recipient has put a card of their own on file, which is the difference between handing someone a year and handing them a bill.

The card is US-only and needs an account billed in US dollars: fine for a nephew in Ohio, useless to a cousin in Toronto.

The Credits: Thirty Days to Claim, Twelve Months to Spend

The second OpenAI product is bought from inside the app by a Plus or Pro subscriber, who picks an amount and receives a one-time claim link to email or paste into a message. Three rules come with it.

It must be redeemed within 30 days of purchase. A gift bought on Black Friday, November 27, 2026 has to be claimed by December 27, 2026 or it lapses and the money returns to the buyer. After redemption the credits last twelve months and do not roll over, with one carve-out: credits redeemed in Japan after November 13, 2026 expire in 180 days instead. The recipient also has to hold an account billed in the same currency as the purchase, because OpenAI will not convert a gift into another currency.

What the credits buy is the part that gets missed. They are prepaid usage, not a plan. A free account can claim them and a Go account can claim them, and neither ends up with Plus. They sit above whatever plan the person already has and are drawn on for metered features once included usage runs out, which in practice means Codex. Send $20 of credits to someone on the free tier and you have funded a few coding sessions, not a month of the product.

An existing credit balance cannot be transferred, gifted or resold. Gifts are new purchases, so a $50 gift is $50 off your card rather than $50 moved out of your account.

Claude: Off Sale Since October

Anthropic sold Claude gifts by email or shareable link, in terms from one month to twelve, until this fall. Its help center now opens with a notice that gift subscriptions are unavailable to purchase, that the pause applies to all accounts, and that there is no date for when buying returns. There is no workaround inside Anthropic's own store.

Two details still matter for anyone holding an unredeemed gift. A Claude gift has to be redeemed within 365 days of purchase, on the web at claude.ai/gift/redeem, with no credit card required. And a recipient with an active iOS or Android subscription has to wait for that subscription to end before the gift can be applied.

The remaining route is Apple's. An Apple Gift Card can pay for a Claude Pro subscription bought inside the iPhone app. Two catches. Apple bills its own in-app price rather than the $20 a month listed on claude.ai, and those two numbers do not always match. The renewal then draws on their Apple balance and, once that is gone, their own payment method.

Microsoft: The Only Card That Buys a Full Year

Microsoft 365 is sold as a 12-month card through US retailers at list price: $99.99 for Personal, $129.99 for Family. Redeem it and the recipient gets a year of Word, Excel, Outlook and 1 TB of OneDrive, with Copilot inside the desktop apps. It is the only card here that buys a fixed term of a year.

The fine print decides which card you buy. On Family, Microsoft's comparison page carries the line AI for subscription owner only. Six people get the Office apps. One person, the owner, gets the Copilot features. Give Family to the person in the house who will actually use the assistant, and do not expect five other accounts to inherit it.

What I Would Buy for Whom

Daily ChatGPT user on the free tier: the $100 card. Five months of Plus, no 30-day window to miss, and the plan continues into their own payment method only if they add one. Nothing about this gift depends on someone opening an email with "gift" in the subject before a deadline. What the free tier stops doing is the argument for Plus in the first place.

Someone who codes with Codex: the gift credits. Credits are the metered currency Codex draws on, so $50 of credit is aimed at the feature that person actually runs out of. The claim window is the risk. Send it to an address they check daily and tell them the deadline out loud.

Someone who writes in Claude: nothing from Anthropic. There is no gift to buy this year. An Apple Gift Card into a Claude Pro subscription bought in the app is the only route that exists, and it puts the renewal on their Apple account. If they work on Android or the web, put the money somewhere else; the fourteen-day free-versus-Pro comparison shows what they would be getting.

A parent who needs Word more than a chatbot: the $99.99 Personal card. Copilot inside Excel and Outlook is what makes it an AI gift, and Personal covers one person. Family at $129.99 only when other people in the house want the Office apps too.

The Verdict

The ChatGPT gift card is the only gift here that funds a subscription, survives a missed email, and stops cleanly when the money runs out. For a US shopper buying for a US recipient, that is the purchase to make on Friday.

The boundaries are real. The card is US-only, so a recipient abroad gets nothing from it. Anyone who lives in Claude has no gift to receive from Anthropic this year, and the Apple route costs them the gap between the App Store price and the web price at every renewal. There is also a case for credits over the card: if the person already pays for Plus, a card balance simply pays renewals they were going to pay anyway, which is a discount on their bill rather than a gift. The year-up-front math covers the version of this decision where you are buying for yourself, what each plan costs per usable hour covers the week you are shopping in, and the refund clocks apply to whoever ends up holding the account.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you gift ChatGPT Plus?

Yes, through the card rather than the credits. OpenAI sells digital cards of $25, $50, $100 and $200 in the US, redeemed at chatgpt.com/redeem into the recipient's balance, which can pay for a subscription, a renewal or Codex credits. The gift credits sold inside ChatGPT are a separate product, prepaid usage rather than a plan, so $20 of them does not buy a month of Plus.

How long does a gifted ChatGPT credit last?

The claim link expires 30 days after purchase, and the money goes back to the buyer if nobody claims it. Once redeemed, the credits last twelve months and do not roll over. Credits redeemed in Japan after November 13, 2026 expire in 180 days instead.

Can you still buy a Claude gift subscription?

No. Anthropic's help center says gift subscriptions are unavailable to purchase for all accounts, with no date for when buying returns. Gifts bought before the pause still redeem within 365 days of purchase at claude.ai/gift/redeem, with no credit card needed, though a recipient with an active iOS or Android subscription has to wait for that subscription to end first.

Does a Microsoft 365 Family gift card give everyone Copilot?

No. The card buys twelve months for up to six people, but Microsoft's comparison page states that AI features are for the subscription owner only. Six accounts get Word, Excel and Outlook, and one account gets the Copilot features inside those apps. Give Family to the person who will use the assistant, or buy Personal at $99.99 for a single recipient.
