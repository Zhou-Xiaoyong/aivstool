Is the Cyber Monday AI Discount Worth It if the Project Ends in March?

The yearly price breaks even in month 9.6, and a March handoff never gets there. Three plans for one launch, $380 across two cards, and the $225 that lands in the month with the least room for it.

I priced this stack on November 28, 2026, two days before the sale closes, from the vendors’ own pages, then handed the same purchase to four assistants and checked their answers against a calculator. The scenario: a launch running December through March, a $60 monthly budget, two cards, and a manager who signs off once per charge of $100 or more.

Three plans cover the work. Claude Pro for the spec and the copy, GitHub Copilot Pro for the code, Runway Standard for the launch video. Bought for the lengths the work actually has, they cost $380 across the twelve months that start December 1. Bought the way the discount pages suggest, with a year on everything that sells one, they cost $464.

Twenty Percent Off Is Priced for a Year You Do Not Have

The annual columns are honest about what they are. Midjourney drops Basic from $10 a month to $8, Runway drops Standard from $15 to $12, and both are a flat 20%. Claude Pro goes from $20 to $16.67 at $200 up front, which is 16.7%. These are the vendors’ standing prices, live in June, live in March, and none of them expires on Monday.

The problem is the denominator. A 20% cut only pays if you keep paying: 0.8 times twelve months is 9.6 months, so every one of these plans breaks even in late month nine, and Claude’s smaller cut takes until month ten. A launch that hands off in March reaches neither date.

Set the two numbers beside each other and the discount inverts. Runway Standard for four months costs $60 monthly or $144 annually. The 20% cut is worth $36 across a full year and costs $84 across four months. Midjourney Basic has the same shape: $40 for four months against $96 for the year. Claude is the worst of the three, because its cut is the smallest: $80 monthly for four months against $200 in one charge.

A yearly price discounts a commitment, not a purchase. Make the commitment four months long and you are buying eight months of a tool nobody opens, at $84.

Whose Card, and What December Looks Like

The card question reads like an expense question and behaves like a renewal question. A monthly plan renews twelve times, and each charge has to survive whatever the card belongs to: a policy, an approval, a job.

My company reimburses anything under $100 a month without approval, and a single charge of $100 or more needs one signature, once per charge. That one line decides the split. Claude’s $200 annual is a single charge, so it is a single signature for the year, which is less work than twelve receipts, and it is $40 cheaper than the monthly route. Copilot at $10 and Runway at $15 sit under the threshold, so they go on my card and get expensed monthly.

The month that falls outside the budget is the one nobody models. December charges $225: $200 of Claude on the company card and $25 of Copilot and Runway on mine. The budget is monthly and the first month is 3.75 times it. Across twelve months the average is $31.67, comfortable, and no approval system on earth looks at averages. Say the $225 out loud when you ask for the signature. The person holding the pen sees one number, and it is not the annual one.

On the company card, leaving is an IT ticket instead of a $200 charge. For a twelve-month commitment, the card should belong to whichever party lives longer.

What Is Actually Left to Buy on Cyber Monday

Two days out, the inventory is short. Nothing on a plan line changes on Monday. We have read these pricing pages repeatedly since early November and the numbers have not moved, because the annual column is not a promotion and was never dated. The year-up-front comparison reached the same conclusion from the discount side.

One offer does expire. Runway carries 25% off Kling 4.0 generations until November 30, which lowers what a rendered clip costs and leaves the $15 plan where it was. Render video this week and that window is real money, closing Monday night. Buy a plan for a March launch and it is a discount on credits you will burn long before the first renewal.

The second deadline is clerical. A charge over the threshold needs a signature, and signatures are slower on the Monday after a four-day weekend than on any other day of the year. If Claude’s annual is the plan, ask today rather than at eleven on Monday night, when the only thing left to lose is the forty-eight hours you still had.

If You Already Paid on Friday

Most buying advice assumes you have not clicked yet. If you have, the useful question is narrower: which line did you buy for the wrong length?

An annual plan bought on Friday is rarely worth unwinding. Canceling and re-buying costs you the days you already paid for and puts a second charge on the same card. If you are inside the refund window, use it; the three vendors measure that window differently, and one of them starts the clock at the request rather than the charge. If you are outside it, give the tool the hardest task of the four months so the year is not entirely speculative.

If It Is Already December

Then the sale is over and nothing was lost, because there was nothing on the plan lines to lose. Start the monthly plans on December 1 and leave the annual question until you know whether the work continues past October.

The date to write down is the renewal. Monthly plans bill on the day you sign up, so a December 2 signup charges again on January 2, then the second of every month after it. That is not a discount question, and the renewal date you cannot move is the part that outlasts whatever you saved.

The Verdict

Annualize Claude Pro at $200 on the company card. Keep Copilot at $10 and Runway at $15 monthly, on your own card, and ignore the yearly price on Runway no matter what label the page puts next to it. Twelve months, $380, one signature, and $225 landing in the month with the least room for it.

The $84 is the whole argument. A 20% cut is worth $36 across a year and costs $84 across four months, and every annual column in this category rests on the same 9.6-month break-even. The tool you will still open in October earns the year. The tool with a March handoff does not, and both sit on the same page at the same discount, which is where the mistake gets made.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Cyber Monday cheaper than Black Friday for AI tools?

Not on a plan price. The annual columns at Claude, Midjourney and Runway are standing prices, not dated promotions, so nothing moves between November 27 and November 30. The one offer that does expire is Runway's 25% off Kling 4.0 generations, which closes November 30 and lowers the cost of a rendered clip rather than the cost of the plan.

Should I buy a yearly AI plan for a project that ends in March?

No. A 20% annual cut breaks even at month 9.6, so four months of use never repays the up-front charge. Runway Standard is $144 for the year against $60 for the four months you need, and Claude Pro is $200 against $80. The discount is real; the length of the commitment is what turns it into a loss.

Does the annual plan belong on a company card or my own?

On whichever card will still exist when it renews. A yearly charge is one approval, which is easier to justify than twelve receipts, and it keeps a $200 renewal off your personal statement if you change jobs. Monthly charges under the approval threshold belong on your own card, where nobody has to sign anything.

My budget is $60 a month and December is $225. Is the plan wrong?

The plan is right and the budget is being read wrong. A monthly budget cannot govern an annual charge, which is why the year averages $31.67 a month while the first month is 3.75 times the cap. Tell the person approving the $225 in advance, because they see one month and not the average.

Can I switch from monthly to annual if the project runs longer?

Yes, on any plan that publishes an annual column. The billing screen offers the yearly price to an active subscription, and the new twelve months start at checkout, which mostly replaces the days left on the monthly cycle rather than refunding them. Read the confirmation line before you pay.
