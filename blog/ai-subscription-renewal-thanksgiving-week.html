Sign Up for AI Tools Thanksgiving Week and You Renew Every Thanksgiving

Six plans priced on the vendors' own pages, $289 leaving in seven days and $89 more before the year turns. The money is easy. The renewal date you cannot move is the part nobody reads.

Six AI subscriptions signed up between November 24 and November 30 charge $289 in November. Four of them charge again between December 24 and December 30, which is $89 more, and $378 before the year is out.

The total is easy to work out. The dates are the part that sticks. Five of these six plans renew on a fixed day of the month or year, so the week you buy in becomes the week you get billed in, permanently.

I priced the basket on November 15, 2026, from the vendors' own pricing pages, then checked every date against a calendar rather than against a model's memory. Then I handed the whole thing to ChatGPT and Claude to see which one could do the arithmetic without slipping a day. That test is at the bottom.

Bottom line: none of the six plans below has a free trial in November 2026. What exists is a free tier with a usage cap and no card on file, which means day one of a paid plan is a charge, not a grace period. Buy monthly for anything you need this weekend and sign up on December 1 for anything you need in January. The price is identical either way. The renewal date is not.

The Short Answer

Buy monthly, and buy late. If a tool is for work you will do between Thursday and Sunday, take the monthly plan and skip every annual option on the page. If the tool is for January, wait until December 1 and let the renewal land on a day you are at a desk instead of in a car.

Two figures carry the decision: $289 of first charges in one week, and $89 of renewals before the year turns. Only the first number is a purchase. The second is a bill you agreed to a month earlier, in a week that had other things going on.

The prices themselves are in our plan-by-plan pricing breakdown, and the discount question is covered separately in what a year up front actually saves. This page is about the calendar.

What the Pricing Pages Say in November 2026

Three vendors, six lines. Checked on November 15 against each vendor's published plan page.

GitHub lists four tiers: Free at $0 with 2,000 completions a month, Pro at $10, Pro+ at $39, Max at $100. Verified students get the Copilot Student plan at no cost. No trial period appears on the paid tiers.

Anthropic lists Free, Pro at $20, Max at $100 and Ultra at $200. Pro can be prepaid at $200 for twelve months, which is $16.67 a month against $240 paid monthly: $40 saved, about 17%. No trial on the paid tiers either.

ChatGPT Plus is $20 a month. Three vendors, three pricing pages, and not one of them offers seven days free on a paid tier.

That absence is the finding. The free trial you keep waiting for was a 2023 pattern. What replaced it is a free tier with a hard usage cap and no stored card, which is better for browsing and worse for deciding, because there is nothing to cancel and therefore nothing that forces a decision.

Five paid charges land inside seven days, and then four renewals land inside another seven, three and a half weeks later. That gap is the problem. It is long enough to forget the first charge and short enough that the second one feels like an error.

Run the identical basket starting December 1 and the first charges move into December while the renewals move into January, and the same $378 still leaves over the same 37 days. What changes is the budget it comes out of: $289 before January 1 instead of $378.

Which is the argument against treating Thanksgiving week as a subscription event at all. A discount changes a price once. A signup date changes every renewal after it, and that is the number nobody reads on the pricing page.

The Date You Cannot Move

Monthly plans renew on the same day of the month. Annual plans renew on the same date of the year. Buy the Claude Pro year on November 29, 2026 and it comes back on November 29, 2027, then again every year you keep it.

A year of statements will not jog the memory. You will open one in December 2027, see $200, and spend twenty minutes reconstructing a Sunday during a football game.

The fix costs four seconds: on the day the first charge clears, put the next one in a calendar. Or cancel at the end of the term and re-buy on a date you will remember. Claude Pro keeps access to the end of the paid period when you cancel, so the exit is clean and you do not lose the last month.

Doing nothing has a cost, and it is not the $200. It is the twenty minutes in December 2027, plus the plan you keep paying for because canceling it needs a decision nobody scheduled.

Four Things to Do Before Thursday

Write the six lines down before you buy any of them. Plan, price, date, renewal. Six rows on one note is enough. Nobody reconstructs this from a statement in January.

Take monthly for anything you will use this weekend. Monthly is a thirty-day decision. Annual is a 365-day one, and the 27th is a poor day to make a 365-day decision.

Take the annual plan only if you would buy it in January anyway. The $40 saved on Claude Pro is real money and also 16.7% of a decision, which is not the same as a reason to make one.

Calendar the four December dates on the day you buy. The 24th, 27th, 28th and 30th collide with travel, gifts and the one week nobody checks email. Our two-week Claude Pro test is the place to start if you are still deciding whether the plan earns its $20.

The Verdict

Winner: the December 1 signup. Same tools, same prices, one to seven days later, and every renewal lands on a date you are at a desk with the statement open.

The exception is real. If the work happens this weekend, buy monthly on the 26th and accept a December renewal. Paying $20 twice within 30 days is cheaper than a $200 prepaid year you cancel in March.

What I would actually pay: Claude Pro monthly at $20 for the weekend, ChatGPT Plus at $20 which is already running, and Copilot Pro at $10 as the cheapest of the paid lines and the one I would keep longest.

Where this stops being a calendar problem: if someone else's accounting department pays, none of it applies. Expense the monthly plan, skip the annual, and let them own the renewal date.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro offer a free trial in 2026?

No. Both vendors list free tiers with usage caps and paid tiers that charge on day one. GitHub lists Free, Pro at $10, Pro+ at $39 and Max at $100, with no trial on the paid plans. Checked on November 15, 2026, none of the three pricing pages offered seven days free on a paid tier, so the first charge is the purchase rather than a grace period.

Is the annual Claude Pro plan actually cheaper?

$200 prepaid against $240 across twelve $20 months, so $40 saved, about 17%. The catch is the date: an annual plan renews on the same date the following year, so a November 29 purchase comes back on November 29, 2027, in the middle of the same busy week.

Which day should I sign up so the renewal misses the holidays?

December 1 or later. A monthly plan bought November 27 renews December 27, inside the same holiday month as the first charge. Buy on December 1 and the second charge lands around New Year's, which is a week you are far more likely to look at a statement.

What about the free GitHub Copilot Student plan?

It costs nothing and still needs verification, which takes a day or more after you submit proof of enrollment. Start it on November 25 rather than Thanksgiving morning if you want the student tier active before the break ends.
