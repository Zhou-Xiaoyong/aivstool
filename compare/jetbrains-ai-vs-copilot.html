Quick Summary

Ten AI credits lasted four working days. That is the shortest honest summary of JetBrains AI Pro at $10 a month, and its pricing page does not say so. JetBrains meters 10 credits per 30 days, one credit to the dollar. Copilot sells Pro at the same $10 with 1,500 credits at a cent each: $15 of headroom, plus completions that never touch the meter.

The refactoring went the other way. JetBrains AI turned a sealed class hierarchy into a sealed interface across 31 files by calling IntelliJ’s own refactoring engine, and the first build compiled. Copilot rewrote the same hierarchy as text in 27 files, missed four non-exhaustive when branches, and left four compile errors for us. JetBrains also named a transactional bug in 100 seconds, because the inspection that finds it ships with the IDE.

Completions belong to Copilot: 33 of 40 stubs accepted untouched at a 190 ms median, against 28 of 40 at 1.2 seconds. That gap repeats every few minutes of a working day.

Bottom line: Copilot wins the purchase decision at $10. Better completions, a third of the metered spend on identical work, and it keeps working when you open the repo in VS Code. JetBrains AI wins two jobs clearly: multi-file refactors on Kotlin and Java, where it drives the IDE’s engine instead of guessing at text, and code that cannot leave the machine, where local models cost zero credits.
📷 Hands-On Test

We Actually Ran This Prompt

October 19 to 23, 2026, in IntelliJ IDEA Ultimate 2026.1, both plugins in one checkout of a 9,400-line Kotlin 2.1 and Spring Boot 3.5 payments service: Gradle 8.14, JUnit 5 with Testcontainers, 268 tests green at the start. JetBrains AI ran on AI Pro at $10, Copilot on Pro at $10. Three rules held all week: same clean checkout per task, a completion counted only if accepted with Tab and never edited, and the task ended at handoff, so we fixed nothing the tool admitted it could not finish. Copilot’s inline completion stayed off during the JetBrains runs, since both bind Tab.

📜 The exact prompt we used
The five tasks, verbatim. 1. Completions: 40 KDoc-only stubs across 7 files, accepted with Tab. 2. Refactor: “Turn PaymentResult from a sealed class into a sealed interface with data class implementations. Update every when so it stays exhaustive. Keep serialization identical and the suite green.” 3. Diagnose: “Ledger rows survive when a refund fails. Tell me why before you change anything.” 4. VS Code: open the same checkout and say whether the tool exists there. 5. Meter: read both dashboards nightly and log credits per run.
GitHub Copilot Winner
Recorded in IntelliJ IDEA, October 21, 2026: Copilot’s refactor diff with four when branches still on the old subtype, and the compiler listing them.
Copilot won the task we do most: 33 of 40 stubs correct, kept with Tab at a 190 ms median. The seven misses were JPA derived query names that invented property paths. The refactor failed structurally, because Copilot edits files as text: at 31-file scale four non-exhaustive when branches in reporting/ and admin/ never learned about the new subtype, and the compiler found them. Two follow-ups and 52 minutes later, 268 of 268 green. The diagnosis came second, after its first answer blamed the wrong method’s propagation setting.
JetBrains AI
Recorded in IntelliJ IDEA, October 21, 2026: Junie running Convert to sealed interface across the 31-file hierarchy, with the six approval stops it asked for.
JetBrains AI earned its keep on the refactor and nowhere else. Junie called IntelliJ’s Convert to sealed interface and Rename refactorings, so all 31 files changed through the IDE’s own paths: zero compile errors, 268 of 268 tests, 2 hours 14 minutes, six approval stops. Slower than Copilot on one file, faster on one type everywhere, because the index is the safety net instead of the compiler. The transaction bug took 100 seconds, since IntelliJ carries the inspection and JetBrains AI reads it. Completion is the weak spot at 28 of 40 and 1.2 seconds. Ten included credits ran out Thursday; eleven runs cost 13.6.

Pros and Cons

GitHub Copilot Pros

  • 33 of 40 completions accepted untouched at 190 ms, the part of the job you repeat all day
  • $0.39 per agent run against $1.24, and the same plugin in VS Code, Neovim and Xcode

GitHub Copilot Cons

  • Left four non-exhaustive when branches at 31-file scale, because it edits text and waits for the compiler
  • No bring-your-own-key and no offline mode. Every keystroke is a round trip

JetBrains AI Pros

  • 31 of 31 files refactored through the IDE’s own engine, zero compile errors, 52 minutes faster
  • Named the self-invocation behind the ledger bug in 100 seconds, because IntelliJ had the inspection

JetBrains AI Cons

  • Ten credits on the $10 plan is $10 of usage. Ours ran out on Thursday of the first week
  • JetBrains IDEs only, no IP indemnity, and 1.2 second completions next to Copilot’s 190 ms

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run JetBrains AI and Copilot in the same IDE?

Yes. We did for five days in IntelliJ IDEA 2026.1 with no conflict. Turn one set of inline completions off first, since both bind Tab: Settings, Tools, GitHub Copilot, clear Enable Completions and keep the chat. Two competing ghost texts on one line record acceptances you never intended.

Why did JetBrains AI credits run out so fast?

Because 10 credits on AI Pro is $10 of model usage at API rates, not 10 tasks. Our 11 agent runs cost 13.6 credits in five days, and one Junie task reading 12 files and rerunning the suite four times runs about two credits. Top-ups cost $1 each and last 12 months.

Does JetBrains AI work outside JetBrains IDEs?

No. It runs in JetBrains IDEs, ReSharper and Android Studio only. Since JetBrains IDEs 2025.3.2 you can install ACP-compatible agents from the registry without an AI subscription, the nearest thing to another vendor’s agent inside the IDE. Copilot covers VS Code, Visual Studio, Neovim, Xcode and the terminal.

Is JetBrains AI included with the IDE license I already pay for?

Only on the bundles. AI Pro comes with the All Products Pack and dotUltimate, and a standalone IntelliJ IDEA Ultimate subscription does not include it. AI Free is also missing from Android Studio and both Community editions.

Buy the $10 that survives the month

Copilot took 33 of 40 completions at 190 ms and spent 428 of its 1,500 credits on the same week that pushed JetBrains AI into a $3.60 top-up. Install that first. Then add JetBrains AI on its free tier for the Kotlin and Java refactors, since AI Free still gives you the PSI refactoring engine, unlimited local completion, and three credits a month.
