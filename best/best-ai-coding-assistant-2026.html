Best AI Coding Assistant 2026

Five assistants, ten tickets from one real backlog, and the number the plan pages hide: what a merged pull request costs once your own review time is on the bill.

Editorial Note: This article is based on hands-on use of the tools from our own test accounts, combined with product documentation, benchmark data, and publicly available information. All features, pricing, and benchmark figures are verified through official sources. See our Disclaimer.

Ten tickets from a 38,600-line TypeScript monorepo, five assistants, fourteen days. The most expensive plan we tested finished first, and it finished $5.48 per merge ahead of a plan that costs less than half as much.

Claude Code on Max at $100 merged nine of the ten tickets. GitHub Copilot on Pro+ at $39 merged eight, at $4.88 a merge in cash. Both numbers are correct, and the gap between them is the argument of this page.

We also timed our own review and priced it at $75 an hour. Adding that column rearranges most of the rankings you have read this year.

Bottom line: Pay for Claude Code on Max if code is what you sell. Nine of ten landed, it stopped 58 times to ask, and the token bill was zero because the $100 covers the pool. Pay for Copilot Pro+ at $39 if you fund your own tools: same eight merges as Cursor for half the money, and $70 of credits that covered every ticket we gave it. Cursor is still the fastest morning you can buy, and still the one that sends a second invoice.

What Changed in 2026

Five assistants, three model families. Opus 5.5, GPT-6 and Gemini 4 are reachable from four of the five tools below, which removes the only thing these products used to compete on. What is left is the meter.

Copilot sells credits. Pro at $10 includes $15 of them, Pro+ at $39 includes $70 and access to Opus, Max at $100 includes $200. Cursor sells usage in multiples of its $8 tier, and that tier runs Grok models only, so Claude starts at $30 with API list price on top. Claude Code arrives with every paid Claude plan and draws on the same pool as chat: a long terminal session and a long conversation spend one allowance.

Two things follow. A $20 plan and a $200 plan can run the same model on the same ticket, and one of them stops at 2 p.m. And the price on the card is a floor. Our Cursor month was $77.30 on a $30 plan.

1. Claude Code on Max: nine of ten

Setup took four minutes. /init wrote a 61-line CLAUDE.md from nine packages. It asked 58 questions across the ten tickets, roughly one every five minutes of agent time, and two of those questions caught a wrong assumption about the Stripe webhook order before any code existed.

Ticket 6 was the one that separated the field. A Playwright test failed about one run in five in CI. Claude Code found a frozen clock shared between two workers and fixed the fixture. Four tools proposed retries. It also added 14 tests we kept, against 6 for Copilot and none for Devin.

It missed ticket 8, the App Router migration, because it wanted to keep getServerSideProps on the two screens with dynamic params. We disagreed with the call. We were right.

2. GitHub Copilot on Pro+: $4.88 a merge

The $70 of credits covered all ten tickets, so the 14-day bill was the plan price. Agent mode in VS Code is slower to start, 6 h 40 m of agent time against 5 h 10 m, and five of its eight merges needed a follow-up commit.

It broke ticket 9 by adding an index to the wrong table. The roster endpoint went from 41 queries to 38. Claude Code got it to four by joining the technician table once.

Pro+ is also the cheapest way we found to run Opus 5 in an editor. $39 with $70 of credits is less than the $100 Claude Max plan, and for a ten-ticket fortnight the credits were enough. Push past them and you buy more credits at GitHub's rate rather than Anthropic's.

3. Cursor on Premium: still the fastest, still a second invoice

3 h 55 m of agent time, the quickest of the five, and the review pane held two tickets at once without turning into a tab hunt. It missed ticket 2, the DST week view, and ticket 6.

The bill is the finding. Third-party models are billed at API list price on top of the $30, and ten tickets on Opus 5.5 cost $47.30. That is $77.30 for the fortnight, more than double the sticker. The $8 Plus tier cannot help, because it runs Grok models only.

4. Aider: the smallest cash number and the longest evenings

$23.80 in tokens across fourteen days is the least money on this page. It is also the most attention: Aider proposes diffs in the terminal and waits, so every rejected change is a dialog you answer while the clock runs.

It merged six. Three of the four misses needed a change outside the file it had opened, and Aider does not go looking unless you tell it to. If you already read diffs for a living, that is a feature. If you do not, it is the reason the loaded cost is $82.09.

5. Devin on Pro: ten sessions, a diff you did not watch

Ten concurrent sessions for $20 is the best paper value here, and it produced seven merges with almost no prompting from us. The cost moves to the other end. 7 h 30 m of review, the most of any tool, because a diff that arrived while you were elsewhere is a diff you read twice.

Our old Windsurf test account now bills through the same page, since windsurf.com/pricing serves Devin's plan table and the editor ships as Devin Desktop. If you were quoted Windsurf pricing in the spring, re-check it.

Where the Money Actually Goes

Devin at $2.86 a merge looks like the bargain of the year until the next column. Seven and a half hours of review against four and a half is $225 of your time at $75 an hour, more than the four subscriptions on this page combined. Aider's $23.80 becomes $82.09.

The two tools that matter to most readers are a dime apart. Copilot Pro+ costs $54.09 a merge loaded, Cursor Premium $54.19, for the same eight merges. Choose between them on how you like to work rather than on price: Copilot lives in the editor you already have, Cursor wants you to move into its own.

Claude Code's $48.61 comes from the merge count, not from the plan. Nine merges spread a fixed $100 further than eight spread $77.30, and the nine minutes saved per merge does the rest.

Final Verdict

Buy Claude Code on Max if you ship code for a living. $100 flat, nine of ten merged, 58 questions that caught two bad assumptions on the webhook ticket, and a token bill of zero. The questions are the product. A tool that edits before it asks is a tool you supervise.

Buy Copilot Pro+ if you fund your own tools. $39 for the same eight merges Cursor delivered at $77.30, with $70 of credits that covered a full fortnight. The install is a VS Code extension, and it delegates to Claude Code and Codex when you want a second opinion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Claude Code worth $100 when Copilot Pro+ is $39?

On merge rate, yes, and on total cost, narrowly. Claude Code merged nine of our ten tickets and Copilot merged eight. Cash per merge was $11.11 against $4.88, but once our review hours are priced at $75 the two land at $48.61 and $54.09, because Claude Code's merges needed less of our evening. If your backlog is small enough that eight is plenty, save the $61 and take Copilot.

Which AI coding assistant is actually free?

Copilot Free gives 2,000 completions a month with Haiku 4.5 and GPT-5 mini and includes the CLI. Cursor Free and Devin Free allow a light quota of agent requests, and Aider is open source with you paying the model provider, which cost us $23.80 in a fortnight. Claude Code is not in Claude's free plan; it starts at Pro, $20.

Why can't the $8 Cursor Plus plan run Claude?

Cursor's own pricing page says Plus includes Grok models only. Third-party models such as Claude and GPT arrive with Premium at $30 and are billed at API list price on top of the subscription. That is how a $30 plan became a $77.30 fortnight for us, and it is the first number to model before you switch editors.

How did you count review time?

A stopwatch, started when the agent handed back the branch and stopped when the ticket was merged or abandoned. It covers reading the diff, running the gates locally, and writing the follow-up instructions. We priced it at $75 an hour, roughly what a US contractor bills, at the same rate for every tool.
