Best AI for SQL Generation in 2026

Six tools, forty questions, one warehouse with duplicated order lines and six currencies. We scored whether the number that came back was the number we asked for.

Six AI tools wrote 40 queries against our nine-table Postgres warehouse. All six produced SQL that executed. On the twelve questions where the schema hid a trap, one tool flagged more than nine of them before writing a line.

SQL generation stopped being a syntax problem a while ago. Ours is a schema problem. order_items picked up 2,847 duplicate rows in a March re-sync, totals sit in a total_minor column beside a currency field holding six values, and every customer, order and subscription carries a deleted_at. A tool that reads the DDL and starts typing will join through those duplicates and hand you revenue that is 6.4% too high, in a calm voice, in four seconds.

Claude Code (Opus 5.5) matched our hand-written reference on 34 of the 40 questions and flagged the ambiguity before writing SQL on 11 of the 12 questions that had one. Gemini 4 Argon answered 31 exactly and won the part nobody benchmarks: all 40 of its queries ran on BigQuery with only the dialect swaps it flagged itself.

The public numbers agree on the ceiling. The AIMultiple text-to-SQL panel put Opus 5.5 at 87.8% reference match, GPT-6.1 Sol at 65.1% and GPT-6 Luna at 44.2%. Human analysts score 92.96 on BIRD and the best system there scores 82.95, so the leader still trails a person on a documented schema.

Bottom line: Buy Claude Code on a $20 Claude Pro seat when the number has to be right the first time. It reads the schema, counts rows to test its own assumption, and asks about the duplicate line items instead of joining through them. Pick Gemini 4 Argon if the warehouse is BigQuery, since 40 of 40 queries ran there unedited at $0.08 a question. Self-host Vanna 2.0 when the schema cannot leave your VPC, and budget about 40 verified queries before it stops guessing.

Why the Schema Decides, Not the Model

Twenty-eight of our forty questions were routine: a filter, a group by, a date range, a rank. The twelve that separated the field hid a decision inside the question, and the decision lived in the schema rather than the prompt. We scored execution, reference match, and whether the tool named the ambiguity before writing SQL. No benchmark measures the third, and Spider 2.0-Snow still errors on 66.1% of questions even though its schemas are documented.

How We Ran It

The test ran October 19 to 23, 2026. The 40 questions came from the analyst who owns our revenue dashboard, written before the test and never shown to a model. The reference answers were hand-written in SQL and kept in a spreadsheet none of the tools could see. Data ran through August 2026.

Every tool got the same 214-line schema dump, the same question order and a fresh session per question. Claude Code and Gemini CLI got a select-only connection to the replica. The three browser tools got the DDL pasted in, which is how most people use them. Nothing was retried, no hints were given, and a clarification a tool asked for counted as a flag, not a second attempt.

The Ranking: Six Ways to Generate SQL

1. Claude Code (Opus 5.5): best accuracy, $20 seat

It read order_items, compared count(*) against count(distinct order_id) on its own initiative, and asked whether the March re-sync was intended before writing the revenue query. That one check is the difference between $387,910 and $412,880 for the year. Thirty-four exact matches, 11 of 12 traps flagged, 41 seconds median per answer including the exploration, and the $20 Pro seat covered all 40 questions inside its weekly cap. Its six misses were ordinary: customers.created_at standing in for activated_at.

2. Gemini 4 Argon: best if the warehouse is BigQuery

Thirty-one exact on Postgres and 40 of 40 portable to BigQuery, where it named every function it had swapped instead of letting you find out at the console. On Postgres it summed total_minor across all six currencies and divided by 100, which makes every Japanese order 100 times too small, because JPY has no minor unit. Cheapest accurate option here at $0.08 a question, and free through Gemini CLI at 1,000 requests a day.

3. ChatGPT (GPT-6.1 Sol): best at repairing SQL you already have

Twenty-eight exact from a pasted schema, which is not the reason it earns a slot. We handed it eight queries written to be bad: a correlated subquery over 4.1 million rows, a NOT IN against a nullable column, a self-join on the wrong key. It rewrote all eight into something that ran and returned the right number, and explained the three-valued logic behind the NOT IN failure without being asked. Median 12 seconds a repair. If your team keeps a folder of queries nobody dares change, this cleans it out.

4. Vanna 2.0 (self-hosted, MIT): best for repeatable self-serve

A framework rather than a chat window. Load the DDL, documentation and verified queries, and it retrieves the closest examples before generating. Cold, with only the schema, it managed 19 of 40. After we loaded 40 golden queries from the dashboard repo it reached 30 of 40 and stopped inventing join paths. Median 3.4 seconds against your own model, no data leaving the VPC, no license fee. Two caveats: the original GitHub repo is archived, so follow the Vanna 2.0 path, and Cloud pricing starts at $50 a month for 20 questions a day.

5. GitHub Copilot Pro ($10): best inside the editor

Twenty-two exact, 4 of 12 traps flagged, and the fastest thing here when you are the one typing: 380 ms completions inside a .sql file with the table names you just used in context. The right $10 if a developer writes the query anyway and wants the join spelled out. It will not audit the schema, and it ignored deleted_at on the active-customer question twice.

6. AI2SQL ($9): best for a one-off query

Paste a schema, get SQL, $9 a month on Starter. Nineteen exact, 2 of 12 traps, no live connection below Pro, and no way to tell it that total_minor needs a currency case. Pro at $24 adds BigQuery, Oracle and MongoDB. A drafting tool for someone who already knows the answer, priced like one.

The Three Things That Broke the Answers

Minor units across six currencies. total_minor stores cents for USD, EUR, GBP, CAD and AUD and whole yen for JPY. Four of the six tools wrote sum(total_minor) / 100 without a currency filter. Japanese revenue is 8.3% of the year and came back at 1% of its real value, and the total looked fine unless you had seen the real figure.

Duplicate order lines. A March re-sync inserted 2,847 line rows a second time with fresh ids. Any query joining orders to items multiplied those lines, and full-year revenue came back 6.4% high. Claude Code caught it by comparing the two counts. Vanna caught it because one of our golden queries carried a distinct. The other four reported the inflated number.

Soft deletes. customers, orders and subscriptions all carry deleted_at. Two tools filtered everywhere, two filtered the main table only, two never mentioned it. An active-customer count that includes deleted accounts reaches a board deck before anyone checks it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can ChatGPT write SQL that is actually correct?

Often the shape, rarely the number. GPT-6.1 Sol matched our reference on 28 of 40 questions and surfaced 7 of the 12 schema traps. Its misses were money and joins: it divided across currencies and joined through duplicated order lines. It was the best tool we tested at repairing a query you paste in, which is a different job from writing one from a question.

What is the best AI for SQL generation in 2026?

Claude Code on Opus 5.5, at 34 of 40 exact matches and 11 of 12 traps flagged before any SQL was written. Gemini 4 Argon is the better buy if your warehouse is BigQuery, since all 40 of its queries ran there with only the dialect swaps it flagged, at roughly a fifth of the cost per question.

Is it safe to point an AI at a production database?

Not at the primary. We used a read replica, a role with select only, a 30-second statement timeout and a 10,000-row cap. The tools that can run their own queries will start a four-table join over 4.1 million rows to test a guess, and a missing where clause on a delete is an expensive way to learn that lesson.

Final Verdict

Claude Code on a $20 Claude Pro seat is the best AI for SQL generation we have tested. Thirty-four of forty exact matches, 11 of 12 schema traps surfaced before any SQL existed, and the habit that matters more than model quality: it counts rows to test its own assumption.

Choose Gemini 4 Argon if the warehouse is BigQuery, because 40 of 40 ran there unedited and $0.08 a question is a rounding error. Self-host Vanna 2.0 if the schema cannot leave your VPC, and budget the 40 golden queries it needs first. Then do what no model here did for us: run select * limit 20 on the table you are about to query. Five minutes of reading beat all six on the questions that mattered.
