Thanksgiving 2026 is Thursday, November 26. Between Wednesday night prep and the Sunday drive home a household gets about fifteen hours it does not normally have, and eleven and a half of those are usable. That second number decides whether a $20 subscription is cheap or expensive.

Thursday contributes nothing and Friday arrives in fragments between the parade and the sales. Divide a monthly price by 11.5 and it stops looking like a rounding error: Claude Max at $100 is $8.70 an hour for a break that cannot use it, and ChatGPT Go at $8 is 70 cents.

We put the cheapest paid tier and the $20 plan on the same Thanksgiving task, read the four vendors' pricing pages on November 6, 2026, and wrote down the cost of each plan per usable hour. One winner, one skip list, and a buying date that matters more than the plan you pick.

Bottom line: Buy Claude Pro at $20 on November 25 and cancel by December 24 if it has not earned it. On our test it built a cookable page in 41 minutes with three prompts, while ChatGPT Go at $8 took 52 minutes and five prompts and left one oven clash in the timeline. If the four days are text and recipes rather than a project, $8 is the right number and the ad slot above the answer is the trade. Cursor Premium, Copilot Pro+ and Claude Max are January purchases: each sells capacity a four-day break cannot reach.

What to Buy Before November 25

Start the clock on the 25th, not the 27th

A monthly plan renews on its purchase date. Buy on November 25 and you hold the plan through December 25, so the break sits inside the first paid month and you still have three weeks to decide about January. Buy on December 1 and the same $20 buys a weekend you have already spent.

A seven-day trial started November 25 runs through December 1

That covers Wednesday to Sunday with two days to spare. Start the same trial on the 24th and it expires on the 30th, Cyber Monday, the one day you might want a paid plan open for comparison shopping. Four days is also short enough that a trial is a real option rather than a countdown you lose track of.

Do not sign an annual plan yet

Claude Pro is the only plan on this list with a published annual price: $17 a month, billed as $200 up front, against $20 monthly. Twelve months at the monthly rate is $240, so the annual saves $40 and only breaks even in month ten. Paying $200 in November for a $40 discount is a ten-month commitment taken in the week you know least about next year. Our longer two-tier test in Is Claude Pro Worth It lands on the same arithmetic.

Skip the second tier

Copilot Pro+ is $39 and adds Opus plus $70 of monthly credits. Claude Max is $100 for five times Pro's allowance. Cursor Premium at $30 is the first Cursor tier that reaches Claude or GPT at all, and those models are billed at API list price on top of it. For four days all three are bills for capacity you cannot reach before Monday. Our Cursor and Copilot pricing test has the per-day numbers if you are choosing between those two for January instead.

What $0 Actually Covers

Three free tiers matter this week, because one of them will finish a small job.

Copilot Free. 2,000 completions a month with Haiku 4.5 and GPT-5 mini, and the CLI is included. A working week burns about 180 a day, so the monthly allowance lasts eleven days. A four-day sprint will not empty it.

ChatGPT Free. Unlimited text chat with capped uploads and capped image generation. The cap that bites over Thanksgiving is uploads: a scanned shopping list and a recipe PDF use two of them before Wednesday.

Claude Free. Sonnet and Haiku only, no Opus and no Claude Code, limited per five hours and per week. This is the tier that stopped us mid-task on a long document in the Free against Plus test in Is ChatGPT Plus Worth It, and it is the tier to avoid if Saturday is the day you need.

If the break is a shopping list and three emails, stop here and spend nothing. The wider comparison of what $0 buys across the category is in our roundup of free AI tools.

The Verdict

Winner: Claude Pro, $20. Three prompts, 41 minutes, nineteen shopping quantities and an oven schedule that never asks one pan to be in two places. The same $20 also carries Claude Code and Opus on the allowance that pays for chat, which is what matters if Saturday becomes a build day rather than a cooking day.

Boundary: if the four days are recipes, a letter and a bit of research, buy ChatGPT Go at $8 and accept an ad slot above the answer. Claude is the wrong product for that job and more than twice the price.

Skip list: Claude Max, Copilot Pro+, Cursor Premium, and any annual plan signed before December.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a Black Friday deal on AI subscriptions in 2026?

No. As of November 6, 2026, OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor and GitHub all showed list prices with no Thanksgiving or Black Friday promotion attached. If one appears on November 27, a monthly plan bought on November 25 has already covered the break, so the decision moves to the December renewal instead of being a guess made now.

Does a subscription bought before Thanksgiving cover the whole break?

Yes, if you buy on or before November 25. A monthly plan renews on the same date the following month, so a November 25 purchase runs to December 25 and the four days sit inside it. Buy on December 1 and the same $20 covers a weekend that has already happened. Claude Pro and ChatGPT Go both bill monthly with no minimum term.

Can I cancel after the four days?

Yes. Cancelling stops the next renewal and the plan stays live until the paid month ends, so cancelling on the day you buy is the safe sequence. None of these vendors refunds part of a month, and Cursor asks for 24 hours' notice before renewal, which means cancelling by December 24 for a plan bought November 25.

Which free tier gets through a four-day project?

Copilot Free if the work is code: 2,000 completions a month with Haiku 4.5 and GPT-5 mini, and the CLI is included. ChatGPT Free if the work is text, though uploads are capped and a scanned recipe uses one. Claude Free is the one to avoid for a Saturday build, since it runs Sonnet and Haiku only, has no Claude Code, and limits use per five hours.
