Black Friday AI Plans in 2026: What a Year Up Front Actually Saves

Six plans, three yearly prices, and the entry tiers we would not lock in for twelve months.

$440. That is what a year of Claude Pro, Midjourney Basic and Runway Standard costs if you pay all three up front on November 27. The same three plans cost $540 in twelve monthly bills. The $100 difference does not reach your account until month ten.

Those three are the only AI subscriptions in our working stack that sell a year at a discount. GitHub Copilot Pro, Cursor Plus and ElevenLabs Starter publish one monthly price and nothing else, so there is no Black Friday cut to wait for on them. We read all six pricing pages between November 6 and November 8, 2026. None of them had a holiday promotion live.

Bottom line: annualize Claude Pro at $200 if you will still be using it in October 2027. Buy Midjourney Basic at $96 or Runway Standard at $144 for the year only if you already generate images or video every month, because a 20% cut pays back at month 9.6 and a spring-only project never gets there. On the three plans with no yearly price, the correct move on Black Friday is to buy nothing.

What Black Friday Actually Cuts

Six pricing pages, three mornings of reading, one price cut that touches a subscription. That cut is not a cut. Runway carries 25% off Kling 4.0 generations until November 30, which lowers what a clip costs in credits and leaves the $15 plan price where it was.

Everything else sits in the annual column, and only three vendors publish one. Twenty percent is the standard. Midjourney drops Basic from $10 to $8 and Standard from $30 to $24. Runway drops Standard from $15 to $12 and Pro from $35 to $28. Anthropic's cut is smaller: $17 a month billed yearly, $200 charged once, which is 16.7% off.

The steepest discount on any of these pages belongs to ElevenLabs, which sells its first month at $1 and then charges $6. That saves $5.

A deals roundup promising half off an AI subscription is describing something that is not on the vendor's page. Our roundup of free AI tools makes the same point from the other end: the $0 tier is real, and most of the paid ladder above it is priced for people who never do the division.

ChatGPT is missing on purpose. Its pricing page would not load when we re-checked on November 8, and we do not print a figure we could not read that morning.

Every 20% annual plan pays back in month 9.6, because 0.8 times twelve months is 9.6 whatever the plan costs. Claude's smaller cut takes ten months, so the discount that looks weaker is the one that demands four extra weeks of commitment.

Divide the up-front total by the monthly price before you decide anything. Our Cursor and Copilot pricing breakdown and the Claude Pro annual calculation both use that division rather than the marketing number, for the same reason.

Run it on a whole stack and the cash question appears. Claude Pro, Midjourney Basic and Runway Standard come to $440 on one statement in one week, against $45 a month if you stay monthly for the year. That is $100 saved and $440 you cannot spend in December, which is not the same decision for a freelancer in a slow January and a shop with a holiday rush.

What to Buy on the 27th

Claude Pro, $200 for the year. Pro is the tier that includes Opus and Claude Code, and it is the plan most of our testers open first. Pay yearly if you cleared month ten last year. At a 16.7% cut, a two-month gap erases the saving.

Midjourney Basic, $96, only with volume. Two hundred jobs a month is 800 images, and Relax is not included, so every re-roll spends a job. If you already generate weekly, $96 is the cheapest route to general commercial terms. If you generate twice a quarter, pay $10 when you need it and keep the other $86.

Runway Standard, $144, only with monthly output. 625 credits is 52 seconds of Gen-4.5 or 78 stills at 1080p, and unused credits do not carry on Standard. One campaign a year never clears month 9.6.

Copilot Pro, Cursor Plus, ElevenLabs Starter: nothing to buy. No yearly price exists on any of the three pages, so keep them monthly and keep the exit, then take ElevenLabs' $1 first month when you actually have a script.

The Verdict

Winner: Claude Pro at $200 a year. One payment, monthly cost down from $20 to $16.67, and a payback at month ten that a daily user clears in the first quarter.

Boundary: if you generate images or video less than once a month, none of these yearly prices clears month 9.6. Pay monthly through the holidays and decide in March, when you know which tools survived the winter.

Skip list: any annual plan whose main limit is a model lock, and any Black Friday AI bundle that does not appear on the vendor's own pricing page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a Black Friday discount on Claude Pro or ChatGPT Plus in 2026?

No promotion was live on either vendor's pricing page when we checked between November 6 and November 8, 2026. Claude Pro does sell a year for $200, which works out to $16.67 a month against $20 monthly, but that price is on the page all year and is not a holiday offer. Treat any advertised percentage that does not appear on the vendor's own page as unverified.

Does annual billing pay off if I only use a tool for six months?

No. A 20% annual cut pays back at month 9.6 and Claude's 16.7% at month 10, so six months leaves you short on either. Annual billing is a bet on your own usage rather than a discount you collect on day one, and the bet is lost quietly because the invoice is already paid.

Why does the $8 Cursor plan lose to a $10 plan?

Cursor Plus runs Grok models only, and its usage pool refreshes weekly rather than monthly. In our 22-day test the pool went dry twice and two tasks were carried into the next month, one of them because the repo's type errors needed a model the plan does not include. At $0.67 a shipped task it was cheaper, and it shipped one task fewer.

Can a vendor change an annual plan after I pay for a year?

Both Anthropic and Cursor reserve the right to change prices and plans, and both pages say so. What you are buying is a price for twelve months, not a feature list for twelve months. Weigh that against 16.7%, which is roughly $40 a year on Claude Pro, and decide whether the discount is worth the loss of the exit.
